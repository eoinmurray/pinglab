Abstract

COBA and PING classifiers share conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics but differ in whether excitatory and inhibitory populations form a reciprocal feedback loop. We derive their event-driven synapses, exponential-Euler membrane update, spike and readout rules, and complete training calculation. We then explain why long recurrent gradient paths can amplify in this model and how voltage-gradient damping stabilizes selected backward sensitivities without changing the forward simulation.

Model family and notation

The COBA and PING classifiers use the same conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire neuron. Their distinction is architectural. The COBA condition disables the recurrent excitatory-to-inhibitory-to-excitatory pathway, whereas PING enables the loop E→I→EE \to I \to EE→I→E.[1]

∇θLtotal=∇θdirectLtotal+∑k=0Nt−1∂Fk∂θ⊤δ[k+1].(36)\nabla_{\theta} L_{\text{total}} = \nabla_{\theta}^{\text{direct}} L_{\text{total}} + \sum_{k = 0}^{N_{t} - 1} {\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial \theta}}^{\top} \boldsymbol{\delta} [ k + 1 ] . \tag{36}∇θ​Ltotal​=∇θdirect​Ltotal​+k=0∑Nt​−1​∂θ∂Fk​​⊤δ[k+1].(36)

Equation 35 and Equation 36 define backpropagation through time (BPTT). PyTorch autograd evaluates these vector–Jacobian products and accumulates parameter gradients when backward propagation is requested; it need not construct the full Jacobian matrices explicitly.[4]

For example, the local recurrence g[k+1]=βg[k]+s[k]W\boldsymbol{g} [ k + 1 ] = \beta \boldsymbol{g} [ k ] + \boldsymbol{s} [ k ] Wg[k+1]=βg[k]+s[k]W gives

Equation 37 shows temporal credit assignment directly: every use of the shared weight contributes according to the later loss sensitivity of the conductance it created.

Surrogate spike derivatives

BPTT requires derivatives of the update operations, but the hard spike in Equation 25 has derivative zero away from threshold and is undefined at threshold. Direct use of that derivative would block useful learning signals.

Surrogate-gradient learning retains the binary forward spike while assigning a smooth backward derivative.[2] For ξ=Vcandidate−Vth\xi = V_{\text{candidate}} - V_{\text{th}}ξ=Vcandidate​−Vth​, the chosen proxy is

p(ξ)=βξ1+β∣ξ∣,∂s∂ξ≈β(1+β∣ξ∣)2.(38)p ( \xi ) = \frac{\beta \xi}{1 + \beta \vert \xi \vert} , \quad \frac{\partial s}{\partial \xi} \approx \frac{\beta}{{( 1 + \beta \vert \xi \vert )}^{2}} . \tag{38}p(ξ)=1+β∣ξ∣βξ​,∂ξ∂s​≈(1+β∣ξ∣)2β​.(38)

The approximate equality in Equation 38 denotes an assigned backward rule rather than the true derivative of the Heaviside function. For hidden neurons, ξ\xiξ is measured in millivolts and β\betaβ in reciprocal millivolts. The standard classifiers use β=1\beta = 1β=1.

Let sg⁡(z)\operatorname{sg} ( z )sg(z) denote stop-gradient: it returns zzz forward but has zero derivative. The simulator constructs

s=sg⁡(H(ξ))+p(ξ)−sg⁡(p(ξ)).(39)s = \operatorname{sg} ( H ( \xi ) ) + p ( \xi ) - \operatorname{sg} ( p ( \xi ) ) . \tag{39}s=sg(H(ξ))+p(ξ)−sg(p(ξ)).(39)

Equation 39 equals H(ξ)H ( \xi )H(ξ) numerically, while autograd differentiates only p(ξ)p ( \xi )p(ξ).[5] Boolean reset and refractory decisions remain non-differentiable. Resetting hidden voltage to a constant cuts that voltage-state path, while the floating spike value carries the surrogate derivative into downstream synapses and the readout.

Why voltage gradients can explode

Expanding Equation 35 across many steps produces repeated Jacobian products:

δ[k]=Jstep[k]⊤Jstep[k+1]⊤…Jstep[Nt−1]⊤δ[Nt]+direct terms.(40)\boldsymbol{\delta} [ k ] = J_{\text{step} [ k ]}^{\top} J_{\text{step} [ k + 1 ]}^{\top} \ldots J_{\text{step} [ N_{t} - 1 ]}^{\top} \boldsymbol{\delta} [ N_{t} ] + \text{direct terms} . \tag{40}δ[k]=Jstep[k]⊤​Jstep[k+1]⊤​…Jstep[Nt​−1]⊤​δ[Nt​]+direct terms.(40)

Products in Equation 40 can shrink useful sensitivities or amplify them. This project combines a 2,000-step graph with voltage, conductance, spike and output-state memory. PING adds a closed route by which a voltage perturbation can return to the population from which it began:

δVE→δsE→δgE→I→δVI→δsI→δgI→E→δVE.(41)\delta \boldsymbol{V}_{E} \to \delta \boldsymbol{s}_{E} \to \delta \boldsymbol{g}_{E \to I} \to \delta \boldsymbol{V}_{I} \to \delta \boldsymbol{s}_{I} \to \delta \boldsymbol{g}_{I \to E} \to \delta \boldsymbol{V}_{E} . \tag{41}δVE​→δsE​→δgE→I​→δVI​→δsI​→δgI→E​→δVE​.(41)

The local gain of this route follows from the membrane equations rather than from an extra model quantity. Choose X=EX = EX=E for excitatory conductance or X=IX = IX=I for inhibitory conductance. Differentiating Equation 18 and the decay aaa in Equation 23 gives

∂V∞∂gX=EX−V∞gtot,∂a∂gX=−ΔtsimCma.(42)\frac{\partial V_{\infty}}{\partial g_{X}} = \frac{E_{X} - V_{\infty}}{g_{\text{tot}}} , \quad \frac{\partial a}{\partial g_{X}} = - \frac{\Delta t_{\text{sim}}}{C_{m}} a . \tag{42}∂gX​∂V∞​​=gtot​EX​−V∞​​,∂gX​∂a​=−Cm​Δtsim​​a.(42)

Applying the product rule to ΔV=(V∞−Vm)(1−a)\Delta V = ( V_{\infty} - V_{m} ) ( 1 - a )ΔV=(V∞​−Vm​)(1−a) gives the conductance-to-voltage response

RX≔∂ΔV∂gX=(EX−V∞)(1−a)gtot+(V∞−Vm)ΔtsimCma.(43)R_{X} \coloneqq \frac{\partial \Delta V}{\partial g_{X}} = \frac{( E_{X} - V_{\infty} ) ( 1 - a )}{g_{\text{tot}}} + ( V_{\infty} - V_{m} ) \frac{\Delta t_{\text{sim}}}{C_{m}} a . \tag{43}RX​:=∂gX​∂ΔV​=gtot​(EX​−V∞​)(1−a)​+(V∞​−Vm​)Cm​Δtsim​​a.(43)

The response RXR_{X}RX​ in Equation 43 has units of millivolts per microsiemens. Let RE→IR_{E \to I}RE→I​ and RI→ER_{I \to E}RI→E​ be diagonal matrices of the appropriate responses at I and E neurons. Let SES_{E}SE​ and SIS_{I}SI​ be diagonal matrices containing the local surrogate derivatives from Equation 38. One complete backward traversal of Equation 41 then has return Jacobian

Jloop=RI→EWI→E⊤SIRE→IWE→I⊤SE.(44)J_{\text{loop}} = R_{I \to E} W_{I \to E}^{\top} S_{I} R_{E \to I} W_{E \to I}^{\top} S_{E} . \tag{44}Jloop​=RI→E​WI→E⊤​SI​RE→I​WE→I⊤​SE​.(44)

The transposes in Equation 44 convert the simulator’s presynaptic-row, postsynaptic-column weight storage into maps acting on column perturbations.

At a fixed local linearization, a sensitivity returning through the loop nnn times contains JloopnδVEJ_{\text{loop}}^{n} \delta \boldsymbol{V}_{E}Jloopn​δVE​. If the spectral radius ρ(Jloop)>1\rho ( J_{\text{loop}} ) > 1ρ(Jloop​)>1, some modes grow geometrically. A singular value above one permits amplification over one traversal, while time-varying, non-normal products can amplify transiently even if each instantaneous eigenvalue has magnitude below one.[3] Inhibitory polarity can reverse a perturbation’s sign without making its magnitude small.

Recurrence therefore does not guarantee exploding gradients. It creates the return operator in Equation 44, which BPTT may traverse many times. Loop-disabled COBA lacks that particular operator, but it still carries gradients through membrane, conductance, spike and readout state across the same long presentation.

Global gradient clipping cannot by itself repair this internal propagation. It acts only after BPTT has formed the complete parameter gradient. Internal sensitivities may already be enormous, non-finite, or dominated by the amplifying paths in Equation 40 before clipping occurs.

Voltage-gradient damping

The project therefore intervenes inside the backward recurrence while preserving the physical forward trajectory. For dimensionless divisor dgrad>0d_{\text{grad}} > 0dgrad​>0, define

αgrad=1dgrad,Dα(z)=αz+(1−α)sg⁡(z).(45)\alpha_{\text{grad}} = \frac{1}{d_{\text{grad}}} , \quad \mathcal{D}_{\alpha ( z )} = \alpha z + ( 1 - \alpha ) \operatorname{sg} ( z ) . \tag{45}αgrad​=dgrad​1​,Dα(z)​=αz+(1−α)sg(z).(45)

Forward evaluation of Equation 45 gives Dα(z)=z\mathcal{D}_{\alpha ( z )} = zDα(z)​=z, but its backward derivative is

∂Dα(z)∂z=α.(46)\frac{\partial \mathcal{D}_{\alpha ( z )}}{\partial z} = \alpha . \tag{46}∂z∂Dα(z)​​=α.(46)

The hidden E and I membrane updates replace Equation 23 by

Vcandidate[k+1]=Vm[k]+Dαgrad(ΔV[k]).(47)V_{\text{candidate} [ k + 1 ]} = V_{m [ k ]} + \mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{\text{grad}}} ( \Delta V [ k ] ) . \tag{47}Vcandidate[k+1]​=Vm[k]​+Dαgrad​​(ΔV[k]).(47)

Equation 45 and Equation 47 leave voltages and spikes unchanged forward. Backward, each selected conductance-to-voltage response is multiplied by αgrad\alpha_{\text{grad}}αgrad​. The PING return term in Equation 44 consequently becomes

Jloop,damped=αgrad2Jloop.(48)J_{\text{loop,damped}} = \alpha_{\text{grad}}^{2} J_{\text{loop}} . \tag{48}Jloop,damped​=αgrad2​Jloop​.(48)

The standard paired classifiers use dgrad=1000d_{\text{grad}} = 1000dgrad​=1000, so αgrad=10−3\alpha_{\text{grad}} = 10^{- 3}αgrad​=10−3 and this particular complete loop term is scaled by 10−610^{- 6}10−6. This does not scale the entire state Jacobian because Equation 45 is inserted only on the ΔV\Delta VΔV branch of Equation 47. For one hidden neuron, the three local derivatives entering its candidate voltage are therefore

∂Vcandidate∂(Vm,gE,gI)=1+αgrad∂ΔV∂Vm,αgrad∂ΔV∂gE,αgrad∂ΔV∂gI.(49)\frac{\partial V_{\text{candidate}}}{\partial ( V_{m} , g_{E} , g_{I} )} = 1 + \alpha_{\text{grad}} \frac{\partial \Delta V}{\partial V_{m}} , \alpha_{\text{grad}} \frac{\partial \Delta V}{\partial g_{E}} , \alpha_{\text{grad}} \frac{\partial \Delta V}{\partial g_{I}} . \tag{49}∂(Vm​,gE​,gI​)∂Vcandidate​​=1+αgrad​∂Vm​∂ΔV​,αgrad​∂gE​∂ΔV​,αgrad​∂gI​∂ΔV​.(49)

Thus the conductance-to-voltage blocks are multiplied by αgrad\alpha_{\text{grad}}αgrad​, but the direct identity route from Vm[k]V_{m [ k ]}Vm[k]​ to Vcandidate[k+1]V_{\text{candidate} [ k + 1 ]}Vcandidate[k+1]​ retains coefficient one. The conductance decay and spike-injection blocks, the refractory-counter update, and the local output recurrence contain no damping operator and are not directly rescaled. Downstream composite derivatives can nevertheless inherit attenuation when their path passes through a damped hidden-voltage increment. There is therefore no scalar αgrad\alpha_{\text{grad}}αgrad​ for which the complete step Jacobian is simply αgradJstep\alpha_{\text{grad}} J_{\text{step}}αgrad​Jstep​.

Holding conductances fixed before bounds and reset, Equation 49 and Equation 23 give

∂Vcandidate[k+1]∂Vm[k]=1−αgrad(1−a).(50)\frac{\partial V_{\text{candidate} [ k + 1 ]}}{\partial V_{m [ k ]}} = 1 - \alpha_{\text{grad} ( 1 - a )} . \tag{50}∂Vm[k]​∂Vcandidate[k+1]​​=1−αgrad(1−a)​.(50)

Without damping, Equation 50 reduces to aaa. Strong damping moves the direct old-voltage derivative toward one while attenuating sensitivities entering through the membrane increment. It can therefore suppress recurrent-drive amplification without simply erasing membrane memory.

Voltage-gradient damping is an altered backward rule, not a biophysical process or the exact derivative of the forward simulator. It deliberately biases temporal credit assignment and may suppress useful sensitivities along with unstable ones. The divisor is therefore an empirical training choice. Standard classifiers apply it to hidden E and I membrane increments, before voltage bounds, spike detection and reset; output-neuron updates are undamped.[1]

After BPTT has accumulated the batch gradient g=∇θLtotal\boldsymbol{g} = \nabla_{\theta} L_{\text{total}}g=∇θ​Ltotal​, the trainer computes its global Euclidean norm

∥g∥2=∑p∑igp,i2.(51)\Vert \boldsymbol{g} \Vert_{2} = \sqrt{\sum_{p} \sum_{i} g_{p , i}^{2}} . \tag{51}∥g∥2​=p∑​i∑​gp,i2​​.(51)

With clipping threshold c=1c = 1c=1, it replaces the gradient by

g~=gmin⁡(1,c∥g∥2).(52)\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}} = \boldsymbol{g} \min ( 1 , \frac{c}{\Vert \boldsymbol{g} \Vert_{2}} ) . \tag{52}g~​=gmin(1,∥g∥2​c​).(52)

m[n]=β1m[n−1]+(1−β1)g~[n],v[n]=β2v[n−1]+(1−β2)g~[n]2.(53)\boldsymbol{m} [ n ] = \beta_{1} \boldsymbol{m} [ n - 1 ] + ( 1 - \beta_{1} ) \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}} [ n ] , \quad \boldsymbol{v} [ n ] = \beta_{2} \boldsymbol{v} [ n - 1 ] + ( 1 - \beta_{2} ) \tilde{\boldsymbol{g}} {[ n ]}^{2} . \tag{53}m[n]=β1​m[n−1]+(1−β1​)g~​[n],v[n]=β2​v[n−1]+(1−β2​)g~​[n]2.(53)

The square in Equation 53 is elementwise. Bias correction gives

m^[n]=m[n]1−β1n,v^[n]=v[n]1−β2n.(54)\hat{\boldsymbol{m}} [ n ] = \frac{\boldsymbol{m} [ n ]}{1 - \beta_{1}^{n}} , \quad \hat{\boldsymbol{v}} [ n ] = \frac{\boldsymbol{v} [ n ]}{1 - \beta_{2}^{n}} . \tag{54}m^[n]=1−β1n​m[n]​,v^[n]=1−β2n​v[n]​.(54)

The standard training recipe used η=0.0004\eta = 0.0004η=0.0004, λwd=0\lambda_{\text{wd}} = 0λwd​=0, minibatches of 256 presentations and 50 epochs. With zero weight decay, Equation 55 is numerically the Adam update for the same hyperparameters.

Dale-constrained trainable connection weights are finally projected onto the non-negative orthant:

θ[n+1]=max⁡(0,θ[n+12]).(56)\theta^{[ n + 1 ]} = \max ( 0 , \theta^{[ n + \frac{1}{2} ]} ) . \tag{56}θ[n+1]=max(0,θ[n+21​]).(56)

The canonical input and readout matrices are trainable, while recurrent E–I matrices are fixed unless an experiment explicitly makes them trainable. Thus one optimization cycle evaluates the forward dynamics, computes Equation 32, applies surrogate-gradient BPTT with the internal rule in Equation 47, clips by Equation 52, updates by Equation 55, and finishes with Equation 56.

Scope and interpretation

The dynamical discretisation is a numerical approximation to the continuous hybrid system, although Equation 9 and Equation 22 exactly solve their respective subproblems under the stated within-step assumptions. Surrogate spike derivatives and voltage-gradient damping introduce additional backward rules: they make learning possible and tractable but do not turn BPTT into the exact derivative of a smooth physical neuron.

The loop derivation isolates one interpretable amplification route rather than the full state Jacobian, and it does not imply that gamma oscillation itself causes gradient explosion. A paired empirical comparison can vary loop engagement and damping independently.
