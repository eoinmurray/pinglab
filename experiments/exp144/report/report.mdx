## Abstract

We compared dense excitatory–inhibitory feedback without background input to
two conditions with 10% reciprocal connectivity and private inhibitory (I)
background excitation at 5 or 10 Hz. Each received the same MNIST image and pixel
spikes for 500 ms from rest at seed 42, with fixed weights and no burn-in.
With 10 Hz background, mean I activity was {rate('win3-i10', 'I')} Hz and mean
10 ms spike-count correlation was {correlation('win3-i10', 'I')}, compared with
{rate('win3-i0', 'I')} Hz and {correlation('win3-i0', 'I')} in the dense control.
This single-image diagnostic shows reduced population synchrony; it does not
establish sustained asynchronous, irregular (AI) activity or classifier performance.

## Results

### Sparse feedback with private I input reduced synchrony

The dense control showed repeated synchronous I bursts. The sparse conditions
had much lower within-I spike-count correlations over the same full 500 ms
window ([Figure 1](#fig-mnist-ai)). Mean excitatory (E) activity was
{rate('win3-i0', 'E')} Hz in the dense control, {rate('win3-i5', 'E')} Hz with
5 Hz I background, and {rate('win3-i10', 'E')} Hz with 10 Hz background.
These comparisons change recurrence and I background together; they do not
isolate the effect of either change.

### Scope of this diagnostic

All three conditions used identical pixel spikes and initial input weights.
No weights were updated. The image is one original training example, not a
held-out evaluation, and only seed 42 was run.

Across the three conditions, {eligibleI} I cells and at most {maxEligibleE} E cell
per condition met the 20-spike requirement for interspike-interval coefficient
of variation (ISI CV). These short recordings therefore provide little evidence
about individual-neuron irregularity. Near-zero count correlation alone does
not establish AI activity. The screen and long protocols were not run, and
historical scratch outputs were not imported as exp144 evidence.
