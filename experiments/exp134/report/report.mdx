## Results

We applied paired interventions to the trained PING (pyramidal–interneuron gamma)
circuit used in [exp123](/exp123), with checkpoint weights from [exp022](/exp022).
Excitatory ($E$) and inhibitory ($I$) populations changed together within each
sweep. The figures describe 37,000 presentations from one checkpoint: 100 test
images, ten per digit, with ten paired encodings each. They do not establish
variation across trained networks or classification performance.

The historical presentation `exp134-r004-present` was derived from
`exp134-r002-analyse` and `exp134-r001-compute`. It used SNNLab 0.18.0 and
100 images. The current recipe defaults to 500 images and the current SNNLab API;
those changes have not produced the displayed evidence.

## Methods

1. **Paired inputs and frozen weights.** We sampled ten images per digit without
   replacement from exp123's balanced 500-image official MNIST test bank, using
   selection seed 134. Pixel intensities in [0, 1] scaled Poisson encoding rates
   up to 25 Hz. Encoding identifiers 42–51 used generator seeds
   $1000k+s$, where $k$ is the original image index and $s$ the encoding identifier.
   Each 200 ms presentation started from fresh state at a 0.1 ms timestep.
   All conditions reused the encodings and final-epoch checkpoint weights.
2. **Separate membrane sweeps (Figures 1–2).** The dimensionless multiplier
   $m_j=0.5\times4^{j/18}$, with setting index $j=0,\ldots,18$, scaled either
   leak conductances $(g_{L,E},g_{L,I})=m_j(0.05,0.10)$ µS or capacitances
   $(C_{m,E},C_{m,I})=m_j(1,0.5)$ nF. The other parameter stayed at its baseline.
   The passive time constant is $\tau_{m,P}=C_{m,P}/g_{L,P}$ in ms for population
   $P\in\{E,I\}$. Thus leak scaling gives $(20,5)/m_j$ ms, and capacitance
   scaling gives $m_j(20,5)$ ms. Both sweeps share the ×1 baseline; inhibitory synaptic decay
   remains 6 ms. These are interventions in the conductance-based model, not
   evidence that passive time constants alone determine the response.
3. **Frequency and firing (panels A, C).** We demeaned full-trial E population
   spike counts and computed a Hann-window Welch power spectral density (PSD)
   with one segment spanning the trial. The peak of the mean PSD in 5–150 Hz
   used parabolic interpolation. Total E spikes/s divides each trial's total
   spike count by 0.2 s, then averages trials; it is not a per-neuron rate.
4. **Participation (panel B).** We smoothed I population counts with a 1 ms
   Gaussian and detected volleys at least 2 ms apart, requiring prominence of
   at least one spike and 20% of the smoothed maximum. Midpoints bracketed
   complete interior cycles. A trial required at least one cycle and mean I
   participation of at least 50%. E participation is the fraction of E neurons
   spiking at least once per accepted cycle, averaged over cycles, accepted draws
   within each image, then eligible images. Every condition accepted all 1,000
   trials and all 100 images; rejected trials would be excluded, not zero-filled.
5. **Rhythm contrast and diagnostics (panels D–G).** SNNLab's normalized
   autocorrelogram used 1 ms bins and lags up to 100 ms. Contrast is
   $(L-T)/(L+T)$, where $L$ is the smoothed central-lobe height before the first
   trough and $T$ that trough's height. We averaged finite trial contrasts.
   Rasters show one diagnostic image and encoding, not an independent evaluation.
