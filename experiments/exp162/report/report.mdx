## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates influence
between neuronal populations ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
This experiment implements the minimal mean-field competition prototype as a
conductance-LIF spiking network. Independent sensory inputs drive two senders,
which converge symmetrically on one receiver. A separate constant-rate cue spike
train changes its delivery target; neither sensory drive nor anatomical connection
strength changes at the switch.

## Discussion

C follows the cued sender in both measured epochs while the uncued sender remains
active and its relative phase slips. The original switching and rhythm criteria
are unchanged. Sparse inputs, local E→I fan-in, lognormal excitatory target gains,
weaker local E→I/I→I weights and a larger network change the recruitment pattern;
this exploration does not isolate their individual causal contributions. Sender→C
I projections remain present at zero execution gain. The native snapshots retain
actual transformed matrices, authenticated against the pinned reference.

I participation remains high; diverse trains do not imply an asynchronous state or biological realism.</p>}

One selected realization does not establish robustness across input seeds or
initial states. The cue changes sender rate and frequency together. No weak
probes, preferential-transmission tests, rate-matched controls or classifier were
run; finite windows do not establish asymptotic locking.
