## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates influence
between neuronal populations ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
This experiment implements the minimal mean-field competition prototype as a
conductance-LIF spiking network. Independent sensory inputs drive two senders,
which converge symmetrically on one receiver. A separate constant-rate cue spike
train changes its delivery target; neither sensory drive nor anatomical connection
strength changes at the switch.

## Discussion

C follows the cued sender in both measured epochs while the uncued sender remains
active and its relative phase slips. Increasing shared sender→C coupling was
sufficient for this example. This is a minimal spiking counterpart to the
mean-field competition prototype, not completion of all plan 4 tests. All cells
within a population share constant weights and identical initial states, producing
perfectly synchronous volleys. Generalization to heterogeneous networks, other
input realizations and different initial states remains untested. The cue changes
sender rate and frequency together. No weak probes, preferential-transmission
tests, rate-matched controls or classifier were run; finite windows do not establish
asymptotic locking.
