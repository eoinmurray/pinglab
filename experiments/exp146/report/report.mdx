asks us to reproduce a single sodium/potassium spiking neuron's response to a
current step in SNNLab, then compare its voltage and spike times with Toolbox
reference traces.

## Methods

### 1. Implement the proposal's equations

The proposal defines a leaky membrane with sodium and potassium currents. For
each channel $x \in \{\mathrm{Na},\mathrm{K}\}$, the gate voltage $z_x$ follows
membrane voltage $V$ with time constant $\tau_x$:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}z_x}{\mathrm{d}t}
= \frac{V-z_x}{\tau_x},
\qquad
g_x = \bar g_x\,\sigma\!\left(\gamma_x(z_x-b_x)\right),
\qquad
\sigma(u)=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-u)}.
$$

Here $t$ is time in ms, $V$ and $z_x$ are in mV, $\tau_x$ is in ms,
$g_x$ is the channel conductance, $\bar g_x$ is its maximum conductance,
$\gamma_x$ is the sigmoid slope in mV$^{-1}$, and $b_x$ is its midpoint in mV.
The sigmoid $\sigma$ maps the dimensionless argument $u$ to a value between
zero and one; $z_x$ itself is a voltage, not a probability.

The membrane equation is

$$
C\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}
= I_{\mathrm{ext}}-g_L(V-V_L)
-g_{\mathrm{Na}}(V-E_{\mathrm{Na}})
-g_{\mathrm{K}}(V-E_{\mathrm{K}}).
$$

Here $C$ is membrane capacitance, $I_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is injected current,
$g_L$ is leak conductance, $V_L$ is the leak reversal potential, and
$E_{\mathrm{Na}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{K}}$ are the sodium and potassium reversal
potentials. We embed the proposal's numerical values in nF, µS and nA, which
are consistent with mV and ms; the proposal does not explicitly label the
capacitance, conductance or current units. Saved values appear in the appendix.

Forward Euler must advance the gates before the membrane. At timestep index
$n$, with step length $\Delta t$, we compute

$$
z_x^{n+1}=z_x^n+\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_x}(V^n-z_x^n),
\qquad
g_x^{n+1}=\bar g_x\,\sigma\!\left(\gamma_x(z_x^{n+1}-b_x)\right),
$$

$$
V^{n+1}=V^n+\frac{\Delta t}{C}\left[
I_{\mathrm{ext}}^n-g_L(V^n-V_L)
-g_{\mathrm{Na}}^{n+1}(V^n-E_{\mathrm{Na}})
-g_{\mathrm{K}}^{n+1}(V^n-E_{\mathrm{K}})
\right].
$$

Superscripts denote sampled timesteps; $I_{\mathrm{ext}}^n$ applies over
$[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t)$. The gate and conductance updates produce panels B
and C, and the membrane update produces panel A. There is no voltage reset.

### 2. Express the dynamics as SNNLab extensions

The implementation below is loaded directly from this experiment's
`custom/dynamics.py`. SNNLab supplies the previous tensor state, timestep,
configuration and incoming currents through `NeuronContext`. The callback
returns the new state and binary upward-crossing events. The initializer
sets voltage and both gates to −70 mV; the synapse passes current unchanged.

The recipe registers these importable callbacks once per process, then uses
`CustomNeuron` and `CustomSynapse` in the authored network. This registration
excerpt exposes the gate voltages and conductances as named state ports:

```python
from snnlab import extensions, lang
from experiments.exp146.custom import dynamics

extensions.register_neuron(
    "pinglab.exp146.toolbox_ei/v1",
    dynamics.step,
    initialize=dynamics.initial_state,
    input_unit="nA",
    state_units={"z_na": "mV", "z_k": "mV", "g_na": "uS", "g_k": "uS"},
    capture_safe=True,
)
extensions.register_synapse(
    "pinglab.exp146.injected_current/v1",
    dynamics.inject_current,
    output_unit="nA",
    capture_safe=True,
)
```

The network contains one custom neuron and a continuous input connected to its
excitatory port through the current extension at unit weight, with direct
initialization scaling. The recipe passes the saved neuron settings as the
custom neuron's configuration. Native graph outputs retain voltage, gates,
conductances, injected current and crossing events.

### 3. Run the current-step protocol and check the traces

Voltage and both gates start at −70 mV. We execute 50,000 steps of
$\Delta t=0.001$ ms, for a total of 50 ms, with float64 arithmetic on CPU.
Injected current is zero before 10 ms and 0.8 nA from 10 ms to the end
(panel D). An upward crossing satisfies $V^n<-20$ mV and $V^{n+1}\geq-20$ mV;
its recorded time is $(n+1)\Delta t$, without interpolation.
