## Introduction

In the pyramidal–interneuron gamma (PING) network of Susin and Destexhe (2021), increasing nonrhythmic excitatory Poisson drive onto both populations shifted an asynchronous-irregular-like baseline toward stronger gamma oscillations generated by recurrent E/I interactions.\[[1](#ref-1)\]

We attempted a modified replication, retaining the reference synaptic decay times, delays and stimulus timing while searching afferent rates and recurrent weights for weak baseline correlations and stimulus-dependent bursting.

Our 2,000-neuron network is smaller and denser but gives each neuron fewer recurrent inputs. Non-adapting LIF neurons replace AdEx neurons; afferent rates and recurrent excitation are lower; inputs are independent across targets; and a 500 ms hidden baseline precedes the display. These changes limit quantitative alignment in firing rates, burst structure or timing.

## Results

<span id="loc-4" />

### Implemented circuit architecture

The circuit contains 1,600 excitatory and 400 inhibitory neurons with recurrent AMPA and GABA synapses ([Fig. 1](#loc-5)). Each neuron receives independent excitatory Poisson input; recurrent I neurons provide inhibition.

### Private drive strengthens bursting

In the selected configuration, a 50% increase in E- and I-targeted afferent rates strengthened population bursting ([Fig. 2](#loc-7)). Mean E/I firing rates rose from 3.53/3.83 Hz at baseline to 11.51/11.93 Hz during the plateau and were 4.28/4.38 Hz in recovery. Mean E spike-count correlation rose from 0.009 to 0.267.

### E and I bursts overlap in time

Excitatory and inhibitory neurons formed aligned bursts during increased drive ([Fig. 3](#loc-9)B). Their plateau spike-count profiles overlap strongly but vary in amplitude and timing ([Fig. 3](#loc-9)C–D); narrow burst bands alternate with dispersed spikes.

**Notes.**

1. Direct external excitation of I may contribute to the overlapping E/I bursts. In an earlier diagnostic using the previous calibration without external I excitation, I peaks followed E peaks by approximately 1 ms across nine plateau bursts.

2. An earlier 1.5-second protocol produced a PING-like burst around 280 ms, before the 500 ms input ramp. Susin and Destexhe likewise report fluctuation-driven spontaneous gamma bursts and residual baseline oscillations.\[[1](#ref-1)\]

The networks share a drive-dependent response but differ in three respects:

1. Increased E/I excitation strengthens collective activity in both; ours has sharper burst bands ([Fig. 4](#loc-11)B–C [Fig. 3](#loc-9)A–B).

2. Their raster samples 1,000 E and 1,000 I neurons from 25,000; ours shows all 1,600 E and 400 I neurons.

3. Both show 600 ms with a 200 ms pulse. Their drive is 2→3→2 Hz during ongoing activity; ours is 0.8→1.2→0.8 Hz after a hidden 500 ms baseline.

Sharper bursts may reflect our non-adapting LIF neurons, altered thresholds and refractory periods, fewer recurrent inputs, recalibrated weights or lower background drive ([Table 1](#loc-14)); sampling also affects raster density. Controlled comparisons are needed.

## Methods

1. **Construct the modified replication.** We replaced the adapting AdEx neurons in the PING network of Susin and Destexhe (2021)\[[1](#ref-1)\] with 1,600 excitatory and 400 inhibitory conductance-based LIF neurons. Recurrent connections, including possible self-connections, were sampled independently with 10% probability. [Table 1](#loc-14) compares the models. We used a 0.1 ms timestep.

2. **Apply the input protocol.** Each neuron received 400 equivalent independent excitatory Poisson afferents, each adding 4 nS of AMPA conductance. We initialized voltages at −65 mV, simulated a 500 ms hidden baseline, then preserved state through the 600 ms display. From displayed time 200 ms, both populations received a 50% rate increase with a 50 ms rise, 100 ms plateau and 50 ms fall ([Fig. 2](#loc-7)E).

3. **Search input and recurrent strengths.** We tested the 27 combinations of baseline afferent rate &#123;0.4, 0.6, 0.8&#125; Hz, recurrent excitatory weight &#123;1.0, 1.25, 1.5&#125; nS and inhibitory weight &#123;2.7, 3.34, 4.0&#125; nS. All combinations used seed 7 and the same recurrent connectivity; those at each input rate shared afferent spike trains.

4. **Measure population activity.** We measured per-neuron firing rates during baseline (0–200 ms), plateau (250–350 ms) and recovery (400–600 ms). Synchrony was the mean pairwise Pearson correlation of 10 ms spike counts among up to 100 evenly sampled neurons per population with nonzero count variance. We calculated median interspike-interval coefficient of variation (CV) among neurons producing at least five spikes, retaining the eligible-neuron count.

5. **Measure burst concentration.** During the 200 ms pulse in each search run, we detected peaks in E-population firing rates using 1 ms bins, Gaussian smoothing with σ = 1 ms, minimum separation of 8 ms, and prominence exceeding the larger of 5 Hz or 20% of the smoothed rate range. Burst participation was the median fraction of E neurons firing within 10 ms windows centred on these peaks.

6. **Select the configuration.** We jointly inspected rasters, rates, correlations and burst participation. We selected 0.8 Hz baseline drive, 1.0 nS recurrent excitation and 3.34 nS recurrent inhibition for weak baseline correlations with clear stimulus-dependent bursting. Selection was exploratory and seed-specific.
