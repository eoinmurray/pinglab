## Abstract

Two saved conductance-LIF states share a network without EE or II recurrence.
One is AI-like; the other shows strong E/I rhythmicity. This migration retains
native spike events, compiled bundles and simulation-start snapshots, with no
new simulation. The states differ in external source rate, input connectivity
and input-to-I gain. Evidence is limited to one selected seed and a two-second
measurement per condition.

## Introduction

The comparison asks whether asynchronous, irregular activity and PING-like
oscillations can occur without within-population recurrence. Both states use
4,000 E neurons, 1,000 I neurons and 4,000 external Poisson sources. The selected
network seed is {data ? String(data.configurations.AI.seed) : 'Unavailable'} in both conditions. These are selected endpoints of a scratch
search, rather than a controlled single-parameter transition.

## Discussion

The selected PING state has a much larger E rhythmicity contrast and a deeply
modulated I population. The recomputed E and I spectral peaks characterise
the shared gamma rhythm. The AI-like
state has low population correlation and near-unit median ISI CVs. Its label
remains descriptive, rather than proof of AI activity across realizations.
