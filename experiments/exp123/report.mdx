  We examine how output evidence accumulates in a trained pyramidal–interneuron
  gamma (PING) network when inhibitory decay changes. With network weights fixed,
  paired MNIST spike encodings are presented at inhibitory decay times of 3, 6
  and 12 ms. We compare cumulative output-spike scores against elapsed time and
  completed inhibitory volleys, then assess sustained correct decisions and
  classification accuracy at matched time, cycle and input-spike coordinates.
  Across three encoding rates, we also compare accuracy, decision deadlines and
  network spike cost, identifying empirical Pareto candidates. These comparisons
  examine whether volley count provides a useful coordinate for evidence
  accumulation. Softmax scores are uncalibrated, later coordinates select trial
  subsets, and spike cost is not a measure of physical energy.

## Evidence across time

<RunFigure experiment="exp123" file="confidence_time.png" number={1} id="fig-time" alt="Nine panels in a three-by-three grid show mean true-class cumulative-spike softmax scores versus elapsed time under three inhibitory-decay settings.">
  A–I each show one of the first nine MNIST test images.
  We converted each image into 100 random spike trains, with brighter
  pixels having higher average spike rates (25 spikes per second for
  a fully bright pixel).
  Each train was presented for 200 ms to the same trained network with
  fixed weights, using three inhibitory decay times: 3 ms (black),
  6 ms (red) and 12 ms (cyan). The x-axis shows elapsed time. The y-axis
  shows the score assigned to the correct digit, calculated by applying
  softmax to accumulated output spike counts. Each curve averages the
  100 trials; no uncertainty bands are shown. This score is not a
  calibrated probability of being correct.
</RunFigure>

We encoded each of the first nine MNIST test images into 100 random
spike trains and reused each train at $\tau_{\mathrm{GABA}} = 3, 6, 12$ ms,
where $\tau_{\mathrm{GABA}}$ is the inhibitory decay time constant.
Brighter pixels had higher average spike rates, with 25 spikes per second
for a fully bright pixel.
Each presentation lasted 200 ms, with 0.1 ms simulation steps. For each
trial, the logit for digit $c$ was its accumulated output spike count:

$$
z_c(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor t / \Delta t_{\mathrm{sim}} \rfloor} s_c[k]. \tag{1}
$$

Here, $t$ is elapsed time, $\Delta t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is the timestep, $k$ indexes
simulation steps, and $s_c[k]$ is one when output neuron $c$ spikes,
otherwise zero. We converted these logits into scores using softmax:

$$
p_c(t) = \frac{\exp(z_c(t))}{\sum_{j=0}^{9} \exp(z_j(t))}. \tag{2}
$$

Here, $p_c(t)$ is the score for digit $c$, and $j$ ranges over all ten
digits. We averaged the correct digit's score *after softmax* across
100 trials, separately for each decay setting. These scores are uncalibrated.

## Evidence per completed volley

<RunFigure experiment="exp123" file="confidence_cycles.png" number={2} id="fig-cycles" alt="The same nine test images as Figure 1 show mean cumulative softmax scores sampled at inhibitory troughs against completed volley count.">
  A–I: the same nine MNIST test images, using the score definition
  and colours of <a href="#fig-time">Figure 1</a>. Each point samples the
  cumulative score at the inhibitory trough after the numbered volley,
  then averages across 100 trials. The trough is the minimum of smoothed
  I activity between successive accepted peaks; the final peak is excluded
  because it has no following boundary.
  Volley zero shows the initial score of 0.1 at time zero, not a detected volley.
  Only counts available in every trial and setting are shown; lines
  connect integer counts without representing elapsed time.
  Clustering and score saturation can mimic alignment.
</RunFigure>

We detected inhibitory peaks after 1 ms Gaussian smoothing, requiring
≥2 ms spacing and prominence ≥max(1 spike, 20% of the maximum).
Accepted sequences required a complete interior cycle and mean
inhibitory participation ≥50%.

We defined each volley's completion as the minimum of smoothed
inhibitory activity between its peak and the next. At that boundary,
we sampled the correct digit's softmax score from all output spikes
accumulated since onset.

We averaged these samples across 100 trials for each decay setting,
using completed-volley counts available in every trial and setting.
The final unbounded volley is excluded; count zero shows the initial
score of 0.1 at time zero, not a detected volley.
Clustering and score saturation can mimic alignment.

## Sustained correct decisions

<RunFigure experiment="exp123" file="sustained_decisions.png" number={3} id="fig-decisions" alt="The same nine images as Figures 1 and 2 show median stable correct decisions versus GABA decay: time in row A and completed volley count in row B, with points connected across decay settings.">

  B uses the first completion at which the decision is already stable.
  Both rows show GABA decay on y for images 00–08. Each point is the

  Failed trials are excluded; B also
  requires completion-boundary coverage within Figure 2's shared count
  range. Trial counts are integers; medians can be fractional.

For the same nine images and 100 trials per decay setting, we found the
earliest update at which the correct digit had the largest cumulative
output spike count and remained the unique winner through 200 ms.
Ties interrupt stability; trials without a stable correct decision were excluded.

We used the first completed-volley trough boundary at or after each
stable decision. This is the first sampled point at which the decision
is already stable, numbered from one.
Counts required accepted volley sequences, a decision no later than the
final completion boundary, and the shared completed-volley count range.
We summarised median decision time and median completed-volley count
against inhibitory decay. The two summaries can include different
trial subsets. Trial counts are integers; medians can be fractional.
Stability was assessed retrospectively, not by a stopping rule.
