## Abstract

The full recurrent shared-input conductance network runs through three windows:
an AI target, an AI-to-PING transition, and a PING target. Only direct external
excitation of inhibitory neurons is reduced; source rate, Input→E gain and all
recurrent weights remain fixed from initialization.
An unfitted four-state mean-field extension also predicts an oscillatory loss of
equilibrium stability as Input→I strength decreases. This single-seed comparison
does not establish an AI state or a PING mechanism.

#### Updated model

The [exp117 closure](/exp117) is extended with E→E and I→I coupling and two
external projections from the same input pool. It retains exp117's four state
variables: population firing rates $r_E,r_I$ in Hz, recurrent excitatory
conductance onto I, $g_e^I$, and recurrent inhibitory conductance onto E,
$g_i^E$, both in µS. With time in ms,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{r,E}\dot r_E&=-r_E+\Phi_E(I_E),\\
\tau_{r,I}\dot r_I&=-r_I+\Phi_I(I_I),\\
\dot g_e^I&=-\frac{g_e^I}{\tau_s}+\frac{G_{E\to I}r_E}{1000},\\
\dot g_i^E&=-\frac{g_i^E}{\tau_s}+\frac{G_{I\to E}r_I}{1000}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\tau_{r,E}=20$ ms and $\tau_{r,I}=5$ ms are the rate-relaxation times,
$\tau_s=5$ ms is the common synaptic decay, and $\Phi_P$ is the stationary
noisy-LIF response in Hz for population $P\in\{E,I\}$. The gain uses exp148's
recorded neuron parameters with exp117's fixed 4 mV voltage-noise scale; its
integral is given in [exp117, Equation A10](/exp117#loc-11). Rates are expressed
in Hz here rather than spikes/ms, accounting for the factors of 1,000.

For source population $P$ and target $Q$, define the summed incoming weight
in µS by

$$
G_{P\to Q}=\frac{1}{N_Q}\sum_{j=1}^{N_P}\sum_{k=1}^{N_Q}W^{P\to Q}_{jk}.
$$

Here $N_P$ and $N_Q$ are source and target population sizes, and
$W^{P\to Q}_{jk}$ is the sampled reference weight from source neuron $j$ to
target neuron $k$. These sums use the six recorded matrices, including their
zeros and excluded autapses.

Within this homogeneous closure, identical outgoing decay times and consistent
initial conductances allow E→E and I→I conductances to be reconstructed without
adding states:

$$
g_e^E=\frac{G_{E\to E}}{G_{E\to I}}g_e^I,
\qquad
g_i^I=\frac{G_{I\to I}}{G_{I\to E}}g_i^E.
$$

Here $g_e^E$ is recurrent excitation onto E and $g_i^I$ is recurrent inhibition
onto I. The cross-population couplings in the denominators are nonzero. The
external pool $X$ supplies stationary mean conductances

$$
\bar g_X^P=\frac{\tau_s G_{X\to P}\nu_X}{1000},\qquad \nu_X=2.5\text{ Hz/source}.
$$

The source rate is $\nu_X$. Only the external Input→I weight multiplier $\alpha$
changes, giving effective currents $I_E,I_I$ in nA:

$$
I_E=\Delta V_e(g_e^E+\bar g_X^E)-\Delta V_i g_i^E,
\qquad
I_I=\Delta V_e(g_e^I+\alpha\bar g_X^I)-\Delta V_i g_i^I.
$$

The excitatory and inhibitory driving forces are $\Delta V_e=70$ mV and
$\Delta V_i=10$ mV, fixed at the resting voltage. This unfitted closure retains
both external mean drives but discards shared-input covariance, shunting,
weight heterogeneity, explicit transmission delays and external filtering lag.
Equilibrium and stability calculations use these four population-rate and
conductance states.

#### Calculations for Figure 2

1. **Panel A — equilibrium rates.** At 201 equally spaced multipliers from
   $\alpha=1$ to zero, set all four time derivatives to zero. Substitute
   $(g_e^I)^*=\tau_sG_{E\to I}r_E^*/1000$ and
   $(g_i^E)^*=\tau_sG_{I\to E}r_I^*/1000$, where the asterisk denotes an
   equilibrium value. Solve the resulting two rate equations
   $r_P^*=\Phi_P(I_P^*)$ by bounded least squares,
   using the preceding equilibrium as the next initial guess. Require a maximum
   rate residual below $10^{-7}$ Hz. Plot the resulting E/I rates against
   $\alpha$; this continues one numerically found branch without establishing
   its global uniqueness.

2. **Panel B — local stability.** Write the four equations as
   $\dot x=F(x;\alpha)$, where $x=(r_E,r_I,g_e^I,g_i^E)$ and $F$ is the vector of
   time derivatives. Evaluate the analytical flow Jacobian
   $J=\partial F/\partial x$ at each equilibrium and plot
   $\max_j\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_j)$, where $\lambda_j$ are its four
   eigenvalues in ms$^{-1}$. Negative values indicate local stability.
   Brent refinement locates the zero at $\alpha=${meanFieldAlpha}. A complex
   pair crosses there while the other pair remains stable. Its linear onset
   frequency is $f=1000|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda)|/(2\pi)=${meanFieldFrequency}
   Hz, converting the critical eigenvalue's imaginary part from radians/ms.
   This tests oscillatory onset without determining Hopf criticality.

3. **Panel C — protocol integration.** Start from the $\alpha=1$ equilibrium,
   add 0.001 Hz to $r_E$, and integrate the four equations through 50 ms burn-in
   and three 750 ms windows. Hold $\alpha=1$, decrease it linearly to zero in
   the middle window, then hold zero. LSODA uses relative tolerance $10^{-8}$,
   absolute rate tolerance $10^{-10}$ Hz and proportionally scaled conductance
   tolerances, preserving the previous error controls under the change of units.
   Use a maximum step of 0.5 ms; sample the solid model
   rate curves every 0.5 ms. For each dashed spiking mean, count spikes within
   that window and divide by population size and its 0.75 s duration. These
   means are comparisons, not fitting targets.

The gain integrals use 48-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature. Independent adaptive
quadrature, finite differences of the flow and a 96-point quadrature check
validate the gain, analytical Jacobian and crossing. Full assumptions and
numerical details are retained in [Appendix D](#mean-field-model). The closure's
oscillatory onset does not establish microscopic AI firing or the spiking
network's PING mechanism.
