## Abstract

We reduce direct external excitation of inhibitory neurons in the shared-input
conductance network used in [exp145](/exp145). The circuit runs continuously
through a baseline, a reduction in external-to-I weights, and a final hold.
Population activity becomes strongly rhythmic during the reduction. This
single-seed observation is consistent with a PING-like transition, but does not
establish the oscillation's mechanism or robustness.

## Results

### Population rhythm emerges as direct I drive falls

The 4,000 excitatory and 1,000 inhibitory neurons retain the six pathways of
exp145, including recurrent excitation and inhibition within each population.
After 50 ms burn-in, the first 500 ms retains baseline input. Over the next
500 ms, X→I weights fall from their initial values to zero. The final 500 ms
holds them at zero. The X→E weights and shared external spike train remain unchanged.
