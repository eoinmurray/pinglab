## Abstract

We compared frozen conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire (COBA-LIF) digit
classifiers with and without pyramidal–interneuron gamma (PING) inhibitory
feedback, using one training image
and identical 100 Hz maximum-rate pixel input. Across discrete timesteps from
0.1 to 0.0125 ms and adaptive continuous-time inference, both selected digit
{label}. Median hidden excitatory voltage error relative to continuous time
changed from {voltageError('coba', 'dt0p1')} to {voltageError('coba', 'dt0p0125')}
mV for COBA and from {voltageError('ping', 'dt0p1')} to
{voltageError('ping', 'dt0p0125')} mV for PING. Continuous refinement preserved
all neuronal and class counts. This single training-image diagnostic does not
estimate held-out accuracy or establish trajectory equivalence.

We compared discrete and continuous-time inference using the frozen scikit-learn

Original image 0 has label {label} and belongs to the

### Classification with matching output dynamics

Each discrete COBA-LIF output layer used its own network's timestep: 0.1, 0.05,
0.025 or 0.0125 ms. In the continuous case, the output neurons evolved
continuously alongside E/I. The saved native graph snapshots verify each
discrete output clock; the continuous solver retains output threshold roots.

Both models selected the true digit in every condition
([Figure 4](#fig-classification)). Continuous COBA predicted
{prediction('coba')}; continuous PING predicted {prediction('ping')}. Class
counts and ranks changed even though the winning digit did not. Total output
spikes remain exp141's trained class score.

Two separate {sourceRate} Hz source controls reproduced every saved exp141
spike and class score exactly. They are retained as validation evidence; all
conditions in Figure 4, including the native 0.2 ms cases, use {inputRate} Hz.
Continuous refinement preserved every neuron's count and all ten class counts;
the largest root shift was {refinementShift} ms. This is one training image,
not a test-accuracy estimate or evidence of equivalent trajectories.

## Methods

1. **Select and verify the source ([Figure 1](#fig-input)).** Pin `exp141-r012-compute` by its payload digest. Load its native
   best-validation simulation-start parameter snapshots, rather than final-epoch
   weights. Replay the original saved
   {sourceRate} Hz input in separate controls and verify every hidden,
   inhibitory and output spike and score against the saved original. Encode
   the same image once at {inputRate} Hz with the native SNNLab rate encoder.
2. **Match physical arrivals ([Figures 2–4](#fig-coba)).** Use a 0.1, 0.05, 0.025 and 0.0125 ms sweep. Replay the same saved
   integer-nanosecond arrivals without rounding or resampling, including each graph's spiking
   output layer at that same timestep. Preserve a physical 0.2 ms E–I delay
   and zero additional E-to-output delay. Retain native 0.2 ms inference
   separately because its slot timing differs.
3. **Integrate continuous dynamics ([Figures 2–4](#fig-coba)).** Integrate every continuous COBA-LIF voltage adaptively, with analytic receptor
   decay, full-weight arrivals, upward threshold roots, hard resets and exact
   refractory clamps. Retain all unrounded spikes and common 0.1 ms samples.
   Native-clock inference remains at 0.2 ms and uses the same 100 Hz input.
   See [Appendix A](#appendix-a--recording-and-clock-conventions) for recording
   and arrival-clock conventions.
4. **Check numerical refinement ([Figure 4](#fig-classification)).** Tighten the tolerances and halve the maximum integration step. Require equal
   per-neuron and class counts, maximum root shift below 0.001 ms and maximum
   per-neuron guarded voltage MAE below 0.001 mV. Both models passed.

The input image, trained weights and spike-count scoring come from exp141.
Inference uses a 100 Hz maximum pixel rate without retraining. Output dynamics
follow the discrete or continuous condition being compared.

### Continuous membrane equation

For membrane voltage $V$ in millivolts and time $t$ in milliseconds, all hidden,
inhibitory and output neurons follow

$$
C\frac{dV}{dt}=g_L(E_L-V)+g_A(E_A-V)+g_G(E_G-V).
$$

$C$ is capacitance in nanofarads; $g_L$, $g_A$ and $g_G$ are leak, AMPA and GABA
conductances in microsiemens. Their reversal potentials $E_L$, $E_A$ and $E_G$ are in millivolts. Receptors decay exponentially between arrivals. Each arrival adds its
frozen weight to the appropriate conductance. Upward threshold crossings emit
spikes, reset voltage and begin a physical refractory clamp; conductances keep
evolving. Output neurons receive AMPA only and have the hidden E-cell physics.
DOP853 integrates voltage between arrivals and threshold events. The parameter
table records nominal and refined tolerances and step limits; recording times
are independent of adaptive integration steps.
