## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates influence
between neuronal populations ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
This minimal extension of the one-sender entrainment examples introduces two
independent senders converging on a shared receiver. Equal sensory drives and
symmetric projections leave the cue as the changing bias. The cue is a separate
spike-input pool represented by its constant mean rate and synaptic filter;
this experiment does not sample individual spikes.

## Discussion

C follows the cued sender more closely in both measured epochs, while the
uncued sender's relative phase slips. Tuning C's fixed background was decisive
for this example. Residual phase modulation remains, and the finite observation
windows do not establish asymptotic locking. The cue changes sender firing rate,
frequency and oscillation amplitude together. No weak probes, rate-matched controls,
transmission tests, cue-strength map or classifier were run. This completes a
minimal competitive-entrainment prototype for plan 3, rather than the full plan.
The mean-field closure omits finite-population fluctuations and voltage-dependent
shunting.
