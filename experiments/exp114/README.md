## Pre-simulation design record

1. Focused question: **Can weak reciprocal excitatory coupling between two compact conductance-based PING modules overcome input-induced frequency detuning, producing an Arnold-tongue-like locking region in which relative phase preserves the signed input difference?**
2. Rationale: Lowet et al. (2015) identify the competition between intrinsic-frequency detuning and coupling as the organizing principle for spatial gamma synchronization, and show that input differences become phase differences inside the locking region. A two-module reduction directly tests that central result while remaining feasible inside the local time and compute limits.
3. Assumptions: each finite PING module can be treated as a noisy oscillator after burn-in; independent stationary Poisson afferents alter each module's preferred frequency; reciprocal E→E and E→I projections are weak relative to local recurrent coupling; Hilbert phase of the band-passed excitatory population rate is meaningful only when both modules show a resolved gamma peak; three fixed seeds quantify finite-network variability but do not establish biological generality.
4. Connections to existing work: `exp023` establishes the repository's baseline conductance-based PING mechanism and input–frequency dependence; `exp041` studies gamma frequency under inhibitory-decay changes; `exp054` defines a separate autocorrelation rhythmicity metric. This experiment adds the missing two-oscillator coupling–detuning comparison and does not reuse trained networks or their outcomes.
5. Fixed design: two identical 60-E/15-I modules receive independent private Poisson afferents. The mean drive is held fixed while the signed drive difference is swept symmetrically. Reciprocal cross-module E→E and E→I coupling is swept from absent to weak; all coupling × detuning × seed cells are retained. Compute records spikes and network definitions; analyse estimates each module's Welch gamma peak, gamma-band power fraction, Hilbert phase concentration and mean signed phase offset after burn-in; present renders exactly three final figures from saved measurements. The deliberately compact populations trade biological scale for completion and make finite-size noise an explicit limitation.
6. Completion criteria fixed before simulation: (a) uncoupled nonzero-detuning cells must show two resolved gamma peaks whose median absolute separation increases relative to zero detuning; (b) at least one nonzero-detuning row must change from unlocked at zero/weak coupling to locked at stronger coupling, where **locked** means median phase concentration at least 0.70 and median absolute peak-frequency difference at most 1.5 Hz, with both module gamma-band power fractions at least 0.20; (c) among locked nonzero-detuning cells, reversing the signed drive difference must reverse the median phase-lead sign; (d) the complete fixed grid and all three seeds must be reported. Failure of any criterion leaves the project incomplete rather than encouraging selective condition search.
