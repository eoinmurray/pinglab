## Question and Circuit

<p>Can a uniform cue select between two simultaneous Gaussian spatial inputs, and why does C sometimes flicker toward the unattended pattern?</p>

<RunFigure report="exp172" experiment="exp172" file="circuit.svg" number={1} id="fig-circuit" alt="Native compiled A/B/C excitatory and inhibitory network with sensory and cue inputs." allowUnavailable={true}>

### Key Details

1. **Spatial input:** A and B receive simultaneous Gaussian bumps in firing rate, centred at different positions on a circular spatial map.
2. **Input→A/B weights:** Fixed sensory weights blend a Gaussian connectivity kernel favouring nearby spatial positions with the original connectivity, preserving each target’s total incoming strength. Weights are not learned.
3. **A/B→C weights:** A→C and B→C use identical fixed Gaussian weight matrices, favouring nearby spatial positions with matched incoming strength. Weights are not learned.
4. **Cue:** A spatially uniform spike input adds {data ? data.selection.cue_hz : 'unavailable'} Hz/channel to the attended sender, switching from A to B at {cfg ? n(cfg.switch_ms) : 'unavailable'} ms. Both sensory inputs remain present.
5. **Local competition:** A, B and C contain excitatory and inhibitory neurons. Input reaching C recruits inhibition, which can suppress a later competing burst.

<p><strong>Local phase locking, $E(t)$</strong></p>

<span id="eq-local-phase-locking" />

$$
E_s(t_k)=\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=k-m+1}^{k}\exp\!\left(i\,2\pi\Delta\phi_{s,j}\right)\right|.
$$

<p>Here $s\in\{A,B\}$ is the sender, $t_k$ is the time of C’s $k$th excitatory peak, $\Delta\phi_{s,j}$ is the sender-minus-C phase in cycles at peak $j$, $m$ is the trailing sample count (here {figure2?.entrainment ? figure2.entrainment.window_cycles : 'unavailable'}), and $i$ is the imaginary unit. The sum includes the current sample; initial or incomplete windows are unavailable. Values near 1 mean a stable phase relationship; values near 0 mean phases spread around the cycle. This is a moving-window <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/%28SICI%291097-0193%281999%298:4%3C194::AID-HBM4%3E3.0.CO;2-C">phase-locking value</a>, not a measure of spatial dominance or proof of causal entrainment. Integer cycle slips are invisible to it; panel A retains that information.</p>

<p><strong>How the receptive phase was measured</strong></p>

- Remove each of {data?.used_window ? data.used_window.packets : 'unavailable'} naturally occurring A/B volleys separately from its input to C. Test every eligible packet in the settled windows, without selecting on dominance.
- Compare each removal with an exact matched continuation from the same native state. Preserve all other inputs, weights and delays; controls reproduce the original spikes.
- Fit C’s spatial pattern over one and two complete cycles. Measure the loss of the removed sender’s pattern and its advantage over the competing pattern. Use the smallest effect across both measures and horizons, normalising the two-cycle result per cycle.
- Shade phase bins with at least {data?.used_window ? data.used_window.settings.minimum_events : 'unavailable'} observed packets and a median effect at least half the positive supported peak. Bin width is 1/{data?.used_window ? data.used_window.settings.phase_bins : 'unavailable'} cycle; the Appendix gives the full method and sensitivity checks.
- This identifies strong transmission phases used in this trial. Weaker effects outside the band remain possible; sparsely sampled phases are unmeasured, not proven inaccessible.

## Metrics

1. <span id="eq-switching-success" /> **Success:**

   $$
   S_e=\frac{N_{\mathrm{correct},e}}{N_e}
   $$

   For cue period $e$, $N_e$ counts retained C cycles and $N_{\mathrm{correct},e}$ counts attended-dominant cycles. Unresolved cycles fail; 1 is perfect.

1. <span id="eq-cycle-flickering" /> **Flickering:**

   $$
   F=\frac{N_{\mathrm{changes}}}{N_{\mathrm{pairs}}}
   $$

   $N_{\mathrm{changes}}$ counts dominance changes across $N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$ adjacent resolved cycle pairs within each cue period. Unresolved cycles break adjacency; no pairs means unavailable. Zero is stable; 1 is continuous alternation.

1. <span id="eq-switching-latency" /> **Latency:**

   $$
   L_e=\min\{k\ge1:z_{e,k}=z_{e,k+1}=z_{e,k+2}=a_e\}
   $$

   Here $z_{e,k}$ is cycle $k$’s dominant pattern and $a_e$ the attended pattern in period $e$. Count complete post-cue cycles, excluding the straddling cycle; report the first of three consecutive correct cycles within the cue period. Unresolved cycles break the run; no run means “Not observed”.

### Explanation

- **Low background may leave C too weakly excited.** The attended sender’s timing advantage may not reliably produce a dominant spatial response.
- **More background could make the attended response more reliable.** Bringing C closer to firing threshold could help an attended volley arriving during low inhibition trigger a response, recruit inhibition and suppress later distractor input. Background supplies excitability; the sender supplies the spatial pattern.
- **Too much background could also hurt.** It could trigger C prematurely or admit distractor input, potentially explaining the trade-off between attention conditions.
- **This remains a mechanistic hypothesis.** Background also changes C’s firing rate and rhythm. Comparing pre-arrival membrane voltage, inhibition and response delay across settings would help distinguish these explanations.
