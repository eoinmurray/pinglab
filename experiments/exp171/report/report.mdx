## Methods

These quantities apply separately to each image and recipient E neuron $j$.

**Parameters taken from the teacher PING**

1. $\mu_j$: temporal mean of inhibitory conductance arriving at E neuron $j$.
2. $v_j$: temporal variance of that inhibitory conductance.

**Derived parameters for artificial I**

1. $q_j$: conductance jump per artificial event,

   $$
   q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j}.
   $$

2. $\lambda_j$: artificial Poisson event rate,

   $$
   \lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)}
   {\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}.
   $$

Here $\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}$, where $\Delta t=0.1$ ms is the timestep
and $\tau_I=6$ ms is the inhibitory decay time. Use seconds for $\Delta t$;
$\lambda_j$ is in events/s. Zero-mean channels receive $q_j=\lambda_j=0$.

**What is matched**

1. Mean inhibitory conductance: $\mathbb{E}[g_j]=\mu_j$.
2. Inhibitory conductance variance: $\operatorname{Var}(g_j)=v_j$.

Here $g_j$ is artificial inhibitory conductance. Matching holds in **stationary
expectation**; finite trials fluctuate. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and
correlations are not matched.

### Detailed procedure

We fitted each image and recipient E neuron separately.

1. **Measure teacher inhibition.** For each E neuron, measure the frozen PING
   teacher's conductance mean and variance over 200 ms:

   <a id="eq-teacher-moments"></a>

   $$
   \mu_j=\operatorname{mean}_n g_j^{P}[n],
   \qquad
   v_j=\operatorname{var}_n g_j^{P}[n]. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $j$ indexes E neurons, $n$ indexes timesteps, and $g^P$ is teacher
   inhibitory conductance; $\mu$ and $v$ are its temporal mean and variance.
   Teacher I spike counts were recorded but not fitted. Teacher and student
   received the same sensory spike train.

2. **Fit artificial input.** Solve for event amplitude $q_j$ and Poisson rate
   $\lambda_j$:

   <a id="eq-poisson-fit"></a>

   $$
   q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j},
   \qquad
   \lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)}{\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}.
   \tag{2}
   $$

   These match the filter's stationary mean and variance; [Appendix A derives
   equation (2)](#derivation-poisson-fit).

   The fixed decay factor was

   <a id="eq-poisson-decay"></a>

   $$
   \beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}. \tag{3}
   $$

   Here $q_j$ is the conductance jump per event and $\lambda_j$ the rate in
   events/s. The timestep is $\Delta t=0.1$ ms and GABA decay time $\tau_I=6$ ms;
   use seconds when calculating rates. Zero-mean targets receive no events.

3. **Generate artificial inhibition.** Give each E neuron an independent Poisson
   channel, filtered through GABA with a one-timestep delay:

   <a id="eq-poisson-input"></a>

   $$
   N_j[n]\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_j\Delta t),
   \qquad
   g_j[n]=\beta g_j[n-1]+q_jN_j[n-1]. \tag{4}
   $$

   Here $N$ counts events and $g$ is artificial inhibitory conductance. Its
   stationary moments are

   <a id="eq-matched-moments"></a>

   $$
   \mathbb{E}[g_j]
   =\frac{q_j\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta}=\mu_j,
   \qquad
   \operatorname{Var}(g_j)
   =\frac{q_j^2\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta^2}=v_j. \tag{5}
   $$

   Fitted rates and amplitudes stayed frozen during fine-tuning; events were
   redrawn each epoch. Mean and variance match in stationary expectation, with
   finite-trial fluctuations. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and temporal or
   cross-neuron correlations are not matched. Biological I is silent; artificial
   inhibition has no E-to-I feedback.

   A 60-ms inhibition-only prehistory initialised conductance. Neurons started
   fresh at stimulus onset. Test-image fits used frozen PING passes without labels
   or weight updates. COBA/PING test scores came from TR02.
