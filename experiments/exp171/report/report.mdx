## Methods

These quantities apply separately to each image and recipient E neuron $j$.

**Parameters taken from the teacher PING**

1. $\mu_j$: temporal mean of inhibitory conductance arriving at E neuron $j$.
2. $v_j$: temporal variance of that inhibitory conductance.

**Derived parameters for artificial I**

1. $q_j$: conductance jump per artificial event,

   $$
   q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j}.
   $$

2. $\lambda_j$: artificial Poisson event rate,

   $$
   \lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)}
   {\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}.
   $$

Here $\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}$, where $\Delta t=0.1$ ms is the timestep
and $\tau_I=6$ ms is the inhibitory decay time. Use seconds for $\Delta t$;
$\lambda_j$ is in events/s. Zero-mean channels receive $q_j=\lambda_j=0$.

**What is matched**

1. Mean inhibitory conductance: $\mathbb{E}[g_j]=\mu_j$.
2. Inhibitory conductance variance: $\operatorname{Var}(g_j)=v_j$.

Here $g_j$ is artificial inhibitory conductance. Matching holds in **stationary
expectation**; finite trials fluctuate. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and
correlations are not matched.

### Detailed procedure

The fit was performed separately for each image and each recipient E neuron j.

1. **Parameters taken from the teacher PING.** From the frozen teacher's
   inhibitory conductance arriving at E neuron j, we measured the temporal mean
   and variance over the 200-ms presentation:

   <a id="eq-teacher-moments"></a>

   $$
   \mu_j=\operatorname{mean}_n g_j^{P}[n],
   \qquad
   v_j=\operatorname{var}_n g_j^{P}[n]. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here, P denotes the PING teacher, n indexes timesteps, and g denotes
   inhibitory conductance. The quantities mu and v are its temporal mean and
   variance, respectively. Teacher I spike counts were recorded separately but
   did not enter this fit. The student received the same sensory spike train.

2. **Output parameters of the fitting.** The fit returned event amplitude q and
   Poisson event rate lambda for each image and E neuron:

   <a id="eq-poisson-fit"></a>

   $$
   q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j},
   \qquad
   \lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)}{\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}.
   \tag{2}
   $$

   The fixed decay factor was

   <a id="eq-poisson-decay"></a>

   $$
   \beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}. \tag{3}
   $$

   Here, q is the conductance jump per event, lambda is the event rate in
   inverse seconds, delta t is the 0.1-ms timestep, and tau I is the 6-ms
   inhibitory decay time. Delta t is expressed in seconds when computing the
   rate. Zero-mean conductance targets received zero rate and amplitude.

3. **What was matched and held fixed.** Each E neuron received independent
   Poisson event counts, filtered through GABA with a one-timestep delay:

   <a id="eq-poisson-input"></a>

   $$
   N_j[n]\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_j\Delta t),
   \qquad
   g_j[n]=\beta g_j[n-1]+q_jN_j[n-1]. \tag{4}
   $$

   Here, N is the event count and g is the artificial inhibitory conductance.
   This discrete filter has stationary moments

   <a id="eq-matched-moments"></a>

   $$
   \mathbb{E}[g_j]
   =\frac{q_j\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta}=\mu_j,
   \qquad
   \operatorname{Var}(g_j)
   =\frac{q_j^2\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta^2}=v_j. \tag{5}
   $$

   Mean and variance were matched in stationary expectation. Each image's fitted
   rate and amplitude remained frozen during fine-tuning; event draws changed
   each epoch. Finite-trial moments fluctuated. Teacher spike rate, timing,
   phase, temporal correlations and correlations between neurons were not
   matched; artificial inhibition supplied no E-to-I feedback. Biological I was
   silent in the student.

   A 60-ms inhibition-only prehistory initialised conductance; neurons started
   afresh at stimulus onset. Test-image fits used frozen PING passes without
   labels or weight updates. COBA/PING test scores were reused from TR02.
