## A. Derivations

For each image and recipient E neuron $j$, fit a conductance jump $q_j$ and
Poisson rate $\lambda_j$ (events/s) to the teacher's conductance moments. Both
stay fixed during training. Equation (2) follows from exact stationary moment
matching for the discrete filter below.

Suppress the neuron index $j$ temporarily. Let $g[n]$ be the artificial inhibitory
conductance at timestep $n$, and $N[n]$ the independent Poisson event count:

<a id="eq-appendix-poisson-filter"></a>

$$
g[n]=\beta g[n-1]+qN[n-1],
\qquad
N[n]\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda\Delta t). \tag{A1}
$$

Here $\Delta t$ and inhibitory decay time $\tau_I$ are in seconds;
$\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}$ is the fixed decay factor. Events arrive one timestep
later. Poisson counts have equal mean and variance:

<a id="eq-appendix-poisson-count-moments"></a>

$$
\mathbb{E}[N[n]]=\operatorname{Var}(N[n])=\lambda\Delta t. \tag{A2}
$$

Let $\mu$ and $v$ be the teacher's temporal conductance mean and variance.
Matching the stationary mean gives

<a id="eq-appendix-stationary-mean"></a>

$$
\mu=\beta\mu+q\lambda\Delta t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu=\frac{q\lambda\Delta t}{1-\beta}. \tag{A3}
$$

New counts are independent of previous conductance, so their covariance is zero.
Matching the stationary variance gives

<a id="eq-appendix-stationary-variance"></a>

$$
v=\beta^2v+q^2\lambda\Delta t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v=\frac{q^2\lambda\Delta t}{1-\beta^2}. \tag{A4}
$$

For $\mu>0$ and $v>0$, dividing variance by mean yields

<a id="eq-appendix-event-amplitude"></a>

$$
\frac{v}{\mu}=\frac{q}{1+\beta}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q=\frac{(1+\beta)v}{\mu}. \tag{A5}
$$

Substituting this amplitude into the mean equation gives

<a id="eq-appendix-event-rate"></a>

$$
\lambda=\frac{\mu(1-\beta)}{q\Delta t}
=\frac{\mu^2(1-\beta)}{\Delta t(1+\beta)v}. \tag{A6}
$$

Restore $j$ to obtain [equation (2)](#eq-poisson-fit): two teacher moments
set two artificial-input parameters at fixed decay. Zero-mean targets receive
no events. The fit rejects constant positive targets ($\mu>0$, $v=0$), which
finite-rate Poisson input cannot reproduce.

Matching is exact in stationary expectation; finite trials fluctuate. The 60-ms
inhibition-only prehistory reduces the initial transient. Teacher spike rate,
timing, phase and correlations remain unconstrained.
