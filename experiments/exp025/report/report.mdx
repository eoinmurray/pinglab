## Abstract

We compared MNIST accuracy and excitatory firing in trained COBA and PING classifiers across activity ceilings, with three independent training seeds per frontier condition. Without a rate penalty in the reduced-pool study, PING reached {ping_off_acc}% test accuracy at {ping_off_rate} Hz, versus {coba_off_acc}% at {coba_off_rate} Hz for COBA. Additional single-seed probes measured cycle participation and input-coupling sensitivity. These comparisons neither establish a structural firing-rate floor nor isolate a benefit of gamma timing or measure energy use.

## Results

### Accuracy-rate activity ceilings

Full-MNIST reference classifiers reached mean final validation accuracies of {full_ping_validation}% for PING and {full_coba_validation}% for COBA ([Figure 1](#fig-1)A–C). In the separate reduced-training-pool comparison, at the unpenalised operating points, PING reached {ping_off_acc}% at
{ping_off_rate} Hz and COBA reached {coba_off_acc}% at {coba_off_rate} Hz. These measurements do not establish a
structural lower firing-rate limit ([Figure 1](#fig-1)D).

## Methods

We reused networks and learning histories from the
[exp022](https://pl.eoinmurray.info/exp022/) — [*MNIST*](https://pl.eoinmurray.info/exp022/) and reanalysed recorded inference
measurements; no new training or simulation was performed.

### Compute

1. **Prepare digit inputs.** Full-MNIST reference classifiers in Figure 1 A–C used
   all 60,000 official-training images, split into 54,000 optimization and 6,000
   validation images. The activity-ceiling and input-coupling studies used a
   separate 7,000-image pool, split into 6,300 optimization and 700 validation
   images. Sampling and class-stratified splitting used seed 42. Pixels drove 784 Poisson channels
   at a 25 Hz maximum, for 200 ms with 0.1 ms steps.

2. **Compare network configurations.** Both conductance-based networks had 1,024
   excitatory (E), 256 inhibitory (I), and 10 output leaky-integrate-and-fire neurons.
   Pyramidal-interneuron gamma (PING) enabled fixed E↔I coupling; COBA disabled it;
   E→E and I→I coupling were zero throughout. Only input and readout weights
   trained; class scores were mean pre-reset output membrane voltages.
   Voltage-gradient damping was 1,000 for both COBA and PING.

3. **Train with activity ceilings.** Networks trained for 50 epochs with AdamW
   (zero weight decay), learning rate $4 \times 10^{-4}$, batch size 256, and
   gradient-norm clipping at 1. Cross-entropy was supplemented by:

   $$
   r_b = 1 / (N_E T_{\mathrm{present}}) \sum_{n \in E} n_{\mathrm{spike}}(b,n), \quad
         L_{\mathrm{rate}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{rate}} / B \sum_b \max(r_b - r_{E,\mathrm{ceil}}, 0)^2. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $n_{\mathrm{spike}}(b,n)$ counts excitatory neuron $n$'s spikes in presentation $b$, $E$ is the
   excitatory population, $N_E$ its size, $T_{\mathrm{present}}$ its duration in seconds, and $B$ minibatch
   size. Rates $r_b$ and ceilings $r_{E,\mathrm{ceil}}$ are in hertz; $\lambda_{\mathrm{rate}} = 0.041$
   $\mathrm{Hz}^{-2}$ weights the dimensionless penalty $L_{\mathrm{rate}}$. Six ceiling conditions
   ([Training settings](#parameters)) and three seeds yielded 36 reduced-pool networks.
   Six separate full-MNIST reference networks trained without an activity penalty;
   their final-checkpoint digit-0 recordings lasted 200 ms, and their learning
   curves averaged the three validation accuracies at each epoch.

### Analyse

4. **Evaluate training endpoints.** Final-epoch weights, rather than
   validation-selected weights, supplied the endpoint comparisons. Each network
   was evaluated on the same 1,000 official-test images; frontier points show
   means and standard errors across seeds 42–44.

5. **Measure cycle participation.** For seed 42, oscillation frequency $f_\gamma$
   was the 5–150 Hz peak of trial-averaged Welch spectra of E activity [1](#ref-welch).
   Participation $p_{\mathrm{part}}$ was the fraction of E-neuron/cycle pairs containing at least
   one spike, with cycles delimited by I-burst midpoints ([Measurement details](#measurement-details)).

   $$
   r_E \approx p_{\mathrm{part}} f_\gamma. \tag{2}
   $$

   This diagnostic approximation relates mean E rate $r_E$ (Hz), dimensionless
   $p_{\mathrm{part}}$, and $f_\gamma$ (Hz); repeated spikes and differing cycle/frequency
   aggregation prevent treating it as an identity.

6. **Vary input coupling.** Twelve PING networks used four initial input-coupling
   means and three seeds, with a 1 Hz ceiling throughout; validation histories
   measured recruitment during training. Separately, seed-42 PING and COBA
   networks trained at 1 Hz were evaluated after multiplying all input weights
   by dimensionless $s \in [0.05, 3]$, holding other weights fixed, on the same
   1,000 test images at 24 scales.

### Present

7. **Expose retained comparisons.** We displayed endpoint comparisons,
   cycle-participation probes and input-coupling sensitivity with their
