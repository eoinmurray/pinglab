## Abstract

Can changes in E→I and I→E strengths alone teach a small circuit which objects to consume?
One initially unbiased inherited circuit (seed 7) undergoes internally driven prenatal
development, juvenile food learning and slower adult learning under a fixed cue–energy mapping.
Food outcomes teach the agent only after consumption. Death restarts development
from the same inherited weights; acquired weights are not passed on.
This run contains {display(lifeSummary.deaths)} deaths
across {display(lifetime.configuration.lifetime_presentations)} encounters.
The phases are a reduced modelling choice; this replay does not test evolutionary
improvement or nature–nurture comparisons.

## Neuron dynamics

Each neuron's normalized activity $r_i(t)\in[0,1]$ relaxes toward a sigmoid of its net input:

$$
\tau_i\frac{dr_i}{dt}
=-r_i+\sigma\!\left(\frac{I_i^E-I_i^I-b_i}{s}\right),
\qquad \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}.
$$

Here $i$ labels the neuron, $t$ is time, and $\tau_i$ sets its response time.
$I_i^E$ and $I_i^I$ are the summed excitatory and inhibitory currents, including
external drive in $I_i^E$; $b_i$ is a bias current and $s$ a current scale.
The sigmoid maps dimensionless input $z$ to activity between zero and one.
E cells provide excitation and I cells provide inhibition.

The input currents are weighted sums:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_i^E(t)&=g\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal E}w_{ji}r_j(t)
+\sum_{k=1}^{3}a_{ki}u_k(t)+\sum_{k=1}^{3}d_{ki}h_k(t)\right],\\
I_i^I(t)&=g\sum_{j\in\mathcal I}w_{ji}r_j(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal I$ are the E- and I-cell sets; $j$ indexes source
cells and $k$ indexes sensory channels. The nonnegative weights $w_{ji}$ connect
source cell $j$ to target cell $i$, while $a_{ki}$ connects sensory channel $k$
to that cell. The sensory values $u_k(t)$ are defined below and are zero prenatally.
The internal values $h_k(t)$ are prenatal pulses and are zero after birth;
$d_{ki}$ is their fixed connection weight to cell $i$.
The scale $g=1\,\mathrm{nA}$
converts dimensionless weighted activity into current. Inhibition enters through
the subtraction of $I_i^I$ in the neuron equation.

For E cells, the recurrent pathways are
EE (E→E) and IE (I→E); for I cells, they are EI (E→I) and II (I→I).
Pathway names put the source first. IE directly suppresses E activity;
II suppresses I activity and can indirectly release E cells from inhibition.
Each 40 ms window starts from the same neuronal activity state; weights carry forward
within a life. This model does not simulate continuous activity between windows.

### Reproduction and selection

The updated protocol selects **inherited settings that produce more offspring**,
while learning adjusts weights within each life. This replaces the earlier
survival-only search; a full reproduction pilot has not yet been run. The replay
above remains the original fixed-genome run.

A living adult becomes eligible to reproduce from age 25, measured in total food
encounters since birth. After each food outcome and learning update, its available
energy determines litter size:

$$
n=\min\!\left(3,\max\!\left(0,\left\lfloor\frac{E-E_{\mathrm{reserve}}}{C}\right\rfloor\right)\right).
$$

Here $n$ is the number of offspring, $E$ is energy after the food outcome and
metabolic cost, $E_{\mathrm{reserve}}=0.5$ is the energy retained by the parent,
and $C=0.5$ is the cost per offspring. At the first eligible encounter with enough
energy, the parent produces one to three offspring and pays $nC$ energy. Each life
permits one reproductive event; an adult without sufficient energy can try again
after a later encounter. These thresholds and costs are fixed modelling choices.

1. **Create the initial population.** Start with the founding genome and
   {display(evolutionSettings.population - 1)} mutated copies, giving
   **{display(evolutionSettings.population)} candidates**.

2. **Evaluate each candidate in {display(evolutionSettings.search_seeds.length)}
   independent lives.** Candidates receive matched food sequences and random inputs.
   Each life starts with freshly instantiated inherited weights, develops prenatally,
   then encounters food until death or **{display(evolutionSettings.horizon)}
   encounters**. There is no rebirth during evaluation.

3. **Rank candidates** by mean offspring produced per life, then mean encounters
   survived, then mean remaining energy, with dead agents assigned zero. Exact ties
   use candidate ID. Prediction accuracy and E/I ratio do not directly influence selection.

4. **Retain up to {display(evolutionSettings.elites)} elite genomes unchanged.**
   Their evaluated lives supply the actual offspring available for the next generation.
   Each offspring has one parent and an independently mutated genome, without its
   parent's learned weights. Admit offspring into the remaining places, sampling
   uniformly if they exceed the population cap of {display(evolutionSettings.population)}.
   Empty places stay empty, so the population can shrink. Retained elites are genomes
   re-evaluated in fresh independent lives, not continuing parental organisms.

5. **Mutate the eight marked parameters.** Weight-distribution means and learning
   rates receive multiplicative perturbations with log-scale standard deviation
   {display(evolutionSettings.log_mutation_std)}. The activity target changes uniformly
   by up to ±{display(evolutionSettings.target_mutation)}; phase durations change by an
   integer from −{display(evolutionSettings.duration_mutation)} to
   +{display(evolutionSettings.duration_mutation)}. Bounds apply, and adult learning
   cannot exceed juvenile learning.

6. **Repeat for up to {display(evolutionSettings.generations)} generations**, counting the
   initial population as generation one. Stop early if the selected parents supply
   no offspring for admission. Finally, compare the winner and founder
   across {display(evolutionSettings.fresh_seeds.length)} additional matched life
   conditions that were excluded from selection.

The full pilot evaluates **up to {display(evolutionSettings.population * evolutionSettings.generations * evolutionSettings.search_seeds.length)}
search lives plus {display(2 * evolutionSettings.fresh_seeds.length)} additional
evaluation lives**. The final comparison tests whether any improvement extends
beyond the conditions used during evolution.

## Prenatal Phase

Before encountering food, the inherited circuit develops through internally generated
activity. In the displayed run, this lasts {display(genome.prenatal_windows)} windows
of {display(lifetime.configuration.presentation_ms)} ms each. Sensory input is zero,
and there are no food outcomes or energy costs.

### Internal activity

Three internal channels generate positive pulses:

$$
h_k(t)=A_k\max\!\left[0,\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi t}{T}+\phi_k\right)\right].
$$

Here $h_k(t)$ is the dimensionless drive on channel $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, $t$ is
time within a window, and $T$ is the window duration,
{display(lifetime.configuration.presentation_ms)} ms. Each window independently
samples amplitude $A_k$ uniformly from 0.3–1 and phase $\phi_k$ uniformly from
zero–$2\pi$. Fixed connections deliver these pulses to both neuronal populations.

### Homeostatic learning

The circuit adjusts its E→I and I→E weights to bring each neuron's average
activity toward an inherited target:

$$
\bar r_i=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}r_i(t_m),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pre}}=\frac12\left[
\frac{1}{N_E}\sum_{i\in\mathcal E}(\bar r_i-\rho)^2+
\frac{1}{N_I}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}(\bar r_i-\rho)^2\right].
$$

Here $r_i(t_m)$ is neuron $i$'s normalized activity at timestep $m$; $M$ counts
timesteps in the window. The sets $\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal I$ contain
$N_E$ excitatory and $N_I$ inhibitory neurons, respectively
{display(lifetime.configuration.e_cells)} and {display(lifetime.configuration.i_cells)}
in this run. The target is $\rho$, set to {display(genome.homeostatic_target_rate)}.
Both populations contribute equally. The prenatal activity meter shows
instantaneous population means in blue (E) and purple (I), with a line at the
target; the loss instead uses each neuron's average over the whole window.

After each window, automatic differentiation supplies gradients for one AdamW
update, using learning rate {display(genome.prenatal_learning_rate)}, no weight
decay and gradient-norm clipping at {display(lifetime.configuration.gradient_clip)}.
Updated weights are clipped to the range zero–{display(lifetime.configuration.weight_upper)};
all other parameters remain fixed.

Neuronal activity restarts from the same state each window, while learned weights
carry forward into subsequent windows and juvenile life. At birth, internal
pulses stop and a fresh optimizer begins food learning. This homeostatic objective
is an engineering modelling choice, not a claimed biological learning mechanism.

## Juvenile Phase

### Food and sensory input

Each object has a cue $x\in[0,1]$ and a hidden energy change
$\Delta E=2x-1\in[-1,1]$. The controller receives the cue through three channels:

$$
\mathbf u(t)=\begin{pmatrix}x\\1-x\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The first two channels support opposite cue preferences; the third supplies
constant drive. Fixed sensory weights project these channels to both E and I
cells, producing the sensory term in $I_i^E$. The cue stays constant throughout
each presentation. Only consumption reveals $\Delta E$ to the learner;
rejected objects provide no outcome feedback.

### Readout

The predicted energy change is a fixed difference between two E-cell groups:

$$
\hat V(x)=G\left[
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar r_i(x)
-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}\bar r_i(x)
\right].
$$

Here $\bar r_i(x)$ is E cell $i$'s mean activity over the final
{display(lifetime.configuration.readout_tail_ms)} ms of the presentation,
$n$ is {display(lifetime.configuration.e_cells / 2)} cells per group,
and the fixed readout gain $G$ is {display(lifetime.configuration.readout_gain)}.
The two displayed readout nodes represent these averaging operations.
The agent consumes if $\hat V(x)>\varepsilon$, where the numerical tolerance
$\varepsilon$ is {display(lifetime.configuration.decision_threshold)}.
Otherwise, it consumes with exploration probability
{percent(lifetime.configuration.exploration_probability)} and rejects the rest.

### Learning rule

After consumption, the revealed energy change supplies a squared-error loss:

$$
\mathcal L=\left[\hat V(x)-\Delta E\right]^2.
$$

Here $\mathcal L$ measures the prediction error. Automatic differentiation through
the neural presentation supplies gradients for the E→I and I→E weight matrices,
$W_{EI}$ and $W_{IE}$. Only these weights learn, in all phases. AdamW takes one step
per consumed object, with no weight decay. Its learning rate is
{display(genome.juvenile_learning_rate)} during juvenile learning and
{display(genome.adult_learning_rate)} in adulthood;
the gradient norm is clipped at {display(lifetime.configuration.gradient_clip)}.
Weights are then clipped to the range zero–{display(lifetime.configuration.weight_upper)}.
Sensory weights, E→E and I→I weights, biases and readout stay fixed.
Rejected objects trigger no update.
The food optimizer starts afresh at birth and retains its moments when the learning
rate drops at adulthood. These gradient-based rules do not claim a biological mechanism.
