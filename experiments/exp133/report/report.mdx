## Results

The paired circuits include all four local E/I pathways, reciprocal excitatory
projections to E and I and separate private input to each circuit. The network
has 14 projections and no attention input.

### Reciprocal coupling and phase locking

Inspired by [Lowet et al. (2015)](#ref-lowet-2015), we tested when two gamma rhythms hold a steady phase difference. Across {result.n_conditions} settings, {result.n_valid} had valid rhythms and {result.n_locked} met all [finite-time locking criteria](#methods); maximum valid PLV was {round(maxPLV, 3)} ([Fig. 1](#fig-tongue)). PLV measures consistency of relative phase, from zero to one; a value near one need not mean zero phase difference. This sampled map describes one realization and does not establish a continuous locking boundary or robustness across seeds.

### Switching on reciprocal coupling

We used the map's −4 Hz input difference and 0.09 μS coupling, keeping its saved weights and connections in both directions. At 500 ms, we switched coupling on. A/B frequencies changed from {round(sr.raster_frequencies.S.first.frequency_hz, 1)}/{round(sr.raster_frequencies.R.first.frequency_hz, 1)} Hz to {round(sr.raster_frequencies.S.last.frequency_hz, 1)}/{round(sr.raster_frequencies.R.last.frequency_hz, 1)} Hz. ## Methods

1. **Construct the paired circuits.** Use the saved conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire (conductance-LIF) networks in [Fig. A1](#fig-network), with parameters in [Table A1](#parameters). Each circuit has 80 E and 20 I neurons and all four local recurrent pathways. The added E→E and I→I pathways have constant weights of 0.00625 and 0.05 μS per connection. Self-connections are excluded, giving incoming totals of 0.49375 and 0.95 μS. Both circuits have 1,024 independent private input channels. No attention input or attention projection is present. Binary input spikes are drawn independently per channel and timestep, with probability equal to input rate times timestep in seconds. Initialize native weights with seed 85 and apply the private-input gain of 9.2 once. No weights are trained.

2. **Sweep reciprocal coupling (Fig. 1).** Cross 11 input differences (−10 to +10 Hz per channel, step 2 Hz) with 13 coupling strengths (0–0.12 μS, step 0.01 μS). Mean input stays 25 Hz per channel. Copy a shared 500 ms uncoupled prefix into each coupling condition, then simulate to 5,000 ms without resetting state. Reuse inputs across coupling strengths and underlying random draws across input differences. Exclude 300 ms after coupling onset. The zero-coupling condition supplies measured frequency detuning. Batched settings are conditions, not replicates.

3. **Measure relative phase (Figs. 1 and 3).** Smooth excitatory population rate with a Gaussian of 1 ms standard deviation; detect peaks separated by at least 15 ms with prominence at least 10% of the maximum smoothed rate. Interpolate phase linearly between consecutive volleys, advancing by $2\pi$ radians per interval; omit samples outside observed volleys. Compute

   $$
   \mathrm{PLV} = \left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} e^{i[\phi_A(n)-\phi_B(n)]}\right|. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $\phi_A(n)$ and $\phi_B(n)$ are circuit phases in radians at shared valid sample $n$, $N$ is the number of those samples, $i$ is the imaginary unit, and bars denote complex magnitude. Estimate frequency as the reciprocal mean inter-volley interval, drift as the least-squares slope of unwrapped relative phase in cycles against seconds, and span as its maximum minus minimum. For the map, each rhythm needs at least 20 volleys, frequency 30–80 Hz and interval coefficient of variation (standard deviation divided by mean) ≤0.2; its uncoupled calibration must also pass. Valid pairs meet the diagnostic locking criteria when absolute drift &lt;0.25 cycles/s, PLV >0.95 and span &lt;1 cycle. These thresholds describe finite windows.

4. **Inspect coupling onset (Fig. 3).** Extract the selected −4 Hz, 0.09 μS map condition over 0–1,500 ms, concatenating its saved uncoupled prefix and coupled suffix without resetting state. Native step gain is zero before 500 ms and one thereafter. Phase metrics use 100–500 ms before coupling and 800–1,500 ms after coupling. Raster frequencies use the first and last 500 ms; each window requires at least five volleys and the same frequency and interval-regularity checks. The onset view uses the map's native matrices and inputs; it is not an independently simulated replicate.
