Our highest PLV was {round(maxPLV, 3)}; {result.n_locked} of {result.n_conditions} settings met the [locking criteria](#parameters). PLV measures how steadily two rhythms hold their phase difference:

$$
\mathrm{PLV} = \left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} e^{i[\phi_A(n)-\phi_B(n)]}\right|. \tag{1}
$$

Here, $\phi_A(n)$ and $\phi_B(n)$ are the rhythms' phases in radians, estimated between bursts of excitatory spikes. $n$ indexes the $N$ shared valid time samples; $i$ is the imaginary unit, and the bars give complex magnitude. PLV ranges from zero to one. A value near one means a nearly fixed phase difference, which need not be zero. It does not prove lasting locking.

## Switching on reciprocal coupling

We used the map's −4 Hz input difference and 0.09 μS coupling, keeping its saved weights and connections in both directions. At 500 ms, we switched coupling on. S/R frequencies changed from {round(sr.raster_frequencies.S.first.frequency_hz, 1)}/{round(sr.raster_frequencies.R.first.frequency_hz, 1)} Hz to {round(sr.raster_frequencies.S.last.frequency_hz, 1)}/{round(sr.raster_frequencies.R.last.frequency_hz, 1)} Hz. Relative phase stopped drifting ([Fig. 3](#fig-sender-receiver)); PLV was {round(sr.windows.coupled.concentration, 3)} and phase span was {round(sr.windows.coupled.phase_span_cycles, 3)} cycles over 800–1,500 ms.
