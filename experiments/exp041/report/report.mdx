## Abstract

We tested whether excitatory firing tracks spectral-peak frequency in eighteen PING classifiers trained at six inhibitory decay times, with three seeds per condition. Across individual networks, frequencies spanned approximately {fgLo}–{fgHi} Hz, E rates {erLo}–{erHi} Hz and test accuracy {accLo}–{accHi}%. Slower rhythms accompanied lower E firing while useful classification persisted. The association is consistent with cycle participation, but does not establish constant participation or identify a physical non-rhythmic baseline.

## Methods

We compared final-epoch dynamics across matched networks trained at different inhibitory decay times, keeping the evaluation data fixed.

1. **Reuse matched trained networks.** Six inhibitory GABA decay constants, $\tau_{\text{GABA}} \in \{ 4.5 , 6 , 9 , 12 , 18 , 27 \}$ ms, and seeds 42–44 defined 18 PING networks. Training used 6,300 MNIST training images and 700 held-out validation images, 50 epochs of AdamW with zero weight decay, learning rate $4 \times 10^{- 4}$, batches of 256, a time-averaged membrane-potential readout and no spike budget. Only inhibitory decay varied between conditions; simulation used 0.1 ms timesteps and 200 ms trials. We reused final-epoch weights to measure endpoint dynamics, without retraining or selecting weights by test performance.

2. **Measure fixed-trial responses.** Each network received the same fixed subset of {cfgEvaluationSamples} images from the official MNIST test partition. We measured classification accuracy, mean excitatory spikes per neuron per second, and each trial’s population-E trace over the full 200 ms. The illustrative raster used image index 0 and seed 42; a fixed random seed of 0 selected displayed neurons without replacement.

3. **Estimate rhythm frequency.** We demeaned each trial’s trace and used a Welch density estimate with one full-trial Hann window \[[1](#ref-1)\], then averaged PSDs across trials. The largest peak between 5 and 150 Hz defined the candidate gamma frequency; its neighbouring linear-power values gave

   $$
   f_{\text{peak}} = f_{\text{peak,bin}} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{y_{0} - y_{2}}{y_{0} - 2 y_{1} + y_{2}} \cdot \Delta f_{\text{bin}} .
   \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $f_{\text{peak}}$ is the interpolated spectral-peak frequency, $f_{\text{peak,bin}}$ the peak-bin frequency, and $\Delta f_{\text{bin}} = 5$ Hz the bin spacing; $y_{0}$, $y_{1}$ and $y_{2}$ are PSD values immediately below, at and above that bin. Because the peak search is restricted to the defined gamma band, the reported estimator is $f_{\gamma} \equiv f_{\text{peak}}$. We clamped the correction to half a bin, using zero offset for zero curvature or a spectrum endpoint. Interpolation reduces bin quantisation but can remain biased \[[2](#ref-2)\]. Per-trial peak distributions were diagnostics; their medians did not enter the fit.

4. **Fit the rate–frequency relation.** We averaged each network’s frequency and excitatory rate over the three seeds, then fitted the six condition points with equal weight by least squares:

   $$
   r_{E} = a + \beta_{r f} \cdot f_{\gamma} , \quad r_{E} = \beta_{r f , 0} \cdot f_{\gamma} .
   \tag{2}
   $$

   Here $r_{E}$ is mean excitatory firing rate in hertz, $a$ is the affine intercept in hertz, and $\beta_{r f}$ and $\beta_{r f , 0}$ are dimensionless fitted slopes. Both fits report $R_{\text{fit}}^{2}$, the coefficient of determination using centred total sum of squares; error bars are sample standard deviations divided by $\sqrt{3}$.

5. **Display matched endpoint evidence.** We displayed the retained frequency, rate and accuracy comparisons across inhibitory decay times, preserving seed-level values and across-seed summaries.
