## Abstract

We plan to compare two-layer conductance-based networks that recognize gestures
represented by visual spike events. COBA provides the feedforward baseline;
PING adds local excitatory–inhibitory feedback. Matched training and held-out
responses will test whether the proposed networks learn and propagate activity.
{run ? 'The paired development measurements are available below.' : 'Training and response measurements remain unperformed.'}
A single training seed and small validation set cannot establish competitive
test performance or a general benefit from inhibition.

## Methods

The architecture and simulation settings are listed in
[Table 1](#architecture-config). We follow the paired training-curve and
held-out-response design of [exp124](/exp124) — [*SHD*](/exp124), with the
following gesture-specific protocol. All training and response procedures
remain planned.

1. **Prepare gesture events.** We will use the training-only Tonic distribution
   of DVS Gesture [1, 2], containing 1,077 segmented examples with floating-point
   timestamps in milliseconds. Fractional timestamps are preserved until binning.
   The two highest-numbered training subjects will supply
   one validation example per class. Selection seed 135 chooses 108 training
   examples from the remaining subjects, with ten examples in nine classes and
   nine in the remaining two classes. The official test set will remain unused.

2. **Encode 500 ms crops.** We retain separate polarities, integer-divide sensor
   coordinates by four to obtain 32 × 32 resolution, and form binary occupancy
   in 1 ms bins. Repeated events in a bin collapse to one spike. Complete source
   event extents are retained in the dataset snapshot. At each training epoch,
   a seeded random integer-millisecond start selects a 500 ms window within each
   gesture. Both networks receive matching crops under the same batch schedule.
   Validation uses a fixed centred 500 ms window per example; examples shorter
   than 500 ms receive zero-input padding. This development validation does not
   measure whole-gesture or multi-window accuracy. Crop encoding uses indexed,
   vectorised event selection through SNNLab's registered encoder interface.

3. **Initialize feedforward weights.** Both networks have 16 excitatory maps at
   32 × 32 followed by 32 maps at 16 × 16, with 3 × 3 AMPA convolutions and
   two 2 × 2 average pools. Pooling carries fractional synaptic drive. Initial
   conductance weights use Gaussian parent mean 0.02 μS and SD 0.004 μS,
   lower-clamped at zero, without fan-in normalization. Parameter-local draws
   with training seed 42 make shared kernels identical across models; we will
   check this equality from the initial weights.

4. **Initialize readout and feedback.** Both models have 11 non-spiking
   leaky-integrator readouts, with signed Gaussian weights of mean zero and
   SD 0.1, identically initialized. PING adds 4 and 8 inhibitory maps and learned
   reciprocal 1 × 1 AMPA/GABA kernels with 1 ms delays. E→I and I→E Gaussian
   parent means are 0.05 and 0.10 μS, respectively, with SD one fifth of the
   mean and lower clamping at zero. Excitatory/inhibitory passive time constants
   are 20/5 ms. These operating weights and timestep remain uncalibrated.

5. **Score each gesture.** Classification uses the mean voltage of each
   non-spiking readout over the 500 ms crop:

   <span id="eq-class-score" />

   $$
   z_c=\frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{k=0}^{N_t-1}V_{\mathrm{out},c}[k],
   \qquad \hat y=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_c z_c .\tag{1}
   $$

   Here $z_c$ is the score for class $c$, $V_{\mathrm{out},c}[k]$ its readout
   voltage in mV at simulation step $k$, $N_t$ the number of crop timesteps (500),
   and $\hat y$ the predicted class. There is no rate normalization, additional
   score gain or voltage centering. The signed readout is phenomenological
   rather than a Dale-constrained biophysical projection.

6. **Train the paired networks.** We will train for twenty epochs with the same
   shuffled example schedule, batch size 54 selected by GPU benchmarking, AdamW, learning rate 0.001,
   zero weight decay, gradient clipping at 1.0 and fast-sigmoid surrogate
   slope 1. Native neuron kernels use compilation. All projection weights are trainable; conductance weights remain
   nonnegative. At batch 54, each epoch contains two optimizer updates; twenty
   epochs therefore contain forty updates. Hidden, readout and synaptic states
   reset between crops.
   Both models use voltage-gradient damping divisor 80 for hidden-neuron
   voltage updates. Training metrics average pre-update measurements across
   examples; validation uses fixed epoch-end weights. The earliest completed
   epoch with minimum example-average validation cross-entropy is selected
   separately for each model. Training will run concurrently on separate GPUs.

7. **Record matched responses.** We will replay the lexicographically first
   selected validation example through each final-epoch network, without
   optimizer updates, and verify identical sample inputs and duration. This
   prespecified sample does not depend on class, correctness or spike appearance.
   We will record every hidden neuron's spikes and the class scores. Final
   weights are used irrespective of the validation-selected epoch.

8. **Calculate population autocorrelograms.** For each hidden population $P$,
   we will count all spikes as $n_P[k]$ in analysis bin $k$ and use SNNLab's
   overlap-corrected, uncentered estimator:

   <span id="eq-autocorrelation" />

   $$
   A_{\mathrm{corr},P}[\ell]
   =\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{N_b-\ell-1}n_P[k]n_P[k+\ell]}
   {(N_b-\ell)\,\bar n_P^{\,2}},
   \qquad
   \bar n_P=\frac{1}{N_b}\sum_{k=0}^{N_b-1}n_P[k].\tag{2}
   $$

   Here $A_{\mathrm{corr},P}[\ell]$ is the normalized autocorrelation at integer
   lag $\ell$, $N_b$ the number of bins, and $\bar n_P$ the mean population
   count per bin. The analysis-bin width and simulation timestep are both
   1 ms; physical lag is $\ell$ milliseconds. We will use positive lags through
   200 ms, omitting zero lag. Silent populations or insufficient overlapping
   bins give undefined values. A unity baseline describes rate-matched
   independent stationary activity, rather than demonstrating that process.
