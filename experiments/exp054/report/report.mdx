## Abstract

We asked where rhythmic activity appears as reciprocal PING coupling activates across the coupling map. We swept coupling directions in a network and compared autocorrelation structure with private- and shared-input controls.

Rhythmic contrast was weak on uncoupled edges and stronger inside the coupled map; shared input imitated contrast without a recurrent loop. This supports the private-input control, but does not establish rate invariance or the spiking transition’s bifurcation type.

## Results

### Coupling-map structure

On either zero-coupling edge the loop was broken: E fired at 168.91 Hz and contrast was 0.000301, while I was silent when E-to-I coupling was zero. The diagonal examples in panels D/E/F had contrasts 0.000301/0.268/0.989. Coupled conditions generally showed stronger temporal structure, although the map was not strictly monotonic ([Fig. 1](#loc-4)).

### Input-sharing controls

Without an inhibitory loop, the private-input null had a maximum contrast of 0.0392 over 2.00–168.91 Hz. The shared-input null reached 1 at 0.0109 Hz, but that estimate contained only 10 E spikes across the population. At 1.39 Hz its contrast was 0.127. Shared afferents can create short-lag coincidence detected by this score; the sparse extreme also illustrates its finite-sample sensitivity. These single-seed controls do not prove rate invariance or establish rhythmicity from contrast alone ([Fig. 4](#loc-10)).

Shared-input examples showed central coincidence without inhibitory feedback; private-input examples showed smaller contrast and finite-sample fluctuations around chance. Their approximate rate matching did not make rates or spike counts equal ([Fig. 5](#loc-12)).

## Methods

1. **Sweep coupling.** We simulated untrained PING networks (1,024 E/256 I neurons) at 11×11 combinations of $W_{E I}=0$–3 µS and $W_{I E}=0$–6 µS. Each E neuron received private 100 Hz Poisson input with identity weight 0.5. Settings were: GABA decay 6 ms; E/I reset holds 1.2/0.6 ms; seed 42; one trial; 0.1 ms steps; 1,000 ms duration, discarding the first 100 ms.

2. **Test input sharing.** With both couplings zero, we scanned private input at 1/2/5/10/20/40/70/100 Hz and shared input at 8/12/16/20/28/40/60/100 Hz. Shared input used 200 channels, weight 0.2 and 95% initial zeros. Reusing the grid's 100 Hz private origin gave 136 unique probes.

3. **Measure rates and autocorrelation.** Per-neuron rates were post-burn spike counts divided by population size and 0.9 s. For E counts $n_{E[k]}$ in $N_{\text{bin}}=900$ bins of width $\Delta t_{\text{bin}}=1$ ms, we calculated

   $$
   A_{\text{corr} ( \ell )} = \frac{1}{{\langle n_{E} \rangle}^{2} ( N_{\text{bin}} - \ell )} \sum_{k = 0}^{N_{\text{bin}} - \ell - 1} n_{E [ k ]} n_{E [ k + \ell ]} .
   \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $A_{\text{corr}(\ell)}$ is normalized autocorrelation, $k$ indexes bins, $\langle n_E\rangle$ is their mean count, and $\ell=1,\ldots,100$ gives lag $\ell\Delta t_{\text{bin}}$. We used zero-padded FFT correlation with overlap and squared-mean normalization: chance reference 1, zero lag excluded.

4. **Calculate lobe–trough contrast.** After filling zero lag from lag 1, we smoothed with weights $(0.25,0.5,0.25)$. We selected the first local minimum from lag 2 and the preceding maximum from lag 1. Their heights $A_{\text{trough}}$ and $A_{\text{lobe}}$ define

   $$
   R_{\text{contrast}} = \frac{A_{\text{lobe}} - A_{\text{trough}}}{A_{\text{lobe}} + A_{\text{trough}}} .
   \tag{2}
   $$

   Contrast $R_{\text{contrast}}$ lies in $[0,1]$ when $0\le A_{\text{trough}}\le A_{\text{lobe}}$ and the denominator is positive. Missing troughs or invalid denominators give undefined scores; no trough floor is applied.
