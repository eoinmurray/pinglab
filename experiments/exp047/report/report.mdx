## Abstract

We asked whether inhibitory population size alters PING activity by adding inhibition or merely redistributing a fixed inhibitory budget. We compared pool-size sweeps that held either summed inhibition or individual synaptic strength fixed.

Firing rates stayed stable under fixed total inhibition but fell as the pool grew when individual synaptic strength was preserved. This supports inverse scaling as a compensation rule for this regime; it does not establish uniqueness or demonstrate gamma rhythmicity.

## Results

<span id="loc-3" />

### Pool-size scaling response

At nominal $G_{I \to E} =$ {gMid} μS, E rates changed from {reFtLoFmt} to {reFtHiFmt} Hz across {nLo}–{nHi} I neurons. At $\bar{w}_{I \to E} \approx$ {jMidNs} nS, E rates fell from {reFsLoFmt} to {reFsHiFmt} Hz and I rates from {riFsLoFmt} to {riFsHiFmt} Hz. All tested fixed-mean levels showed this decrease, consistent with stronger summed inhibition and reduced E–I feedback ([Fig. 1](#loc-4)).

## Methods

We compared two scaling controls using recorded outputs from simulations of untrained, dense recurrent excitatory–inhibitory networks, without additional simulation.

1. **Initialize fan-in-normalized weights.** For an I→E matrix with {cfgNE} excitatory columns and $N_{I}$ inhibitory rows, each weight was $W_{k j}^{I E} = \frac{G_{\text{draw}}}{N_{I}}$, with $G_{\text{draw}} = \max ( 0 , X )$ and $X \sim \mathcal{N} ( \mu_{\text{init}} , \sigma_{\text{init}}^{2} )$ a Gaussian draw of mean $\mu_{\text{init}}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{init}} = 0.1 \mu_{\text{init}}$, in μS. Defining expected summed coupling $G_{I \to E} = \mathcal{E} [ G_{\text{draw}} ]$ gives

   $$
   \mathcal{E} [ W_{k j}^{I E} ] = \bar{w}_{I \to E} = \frac{G_{I \to E}}{N_{I}} , \quad \mathcal{E} [ \sum_{k = 1}^{N_{I}} W_{k j}^{I E} ] = G_{I \to E} .
   \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $\mathcal{E}$ averages over weight initialization, $k$ indexes inhibitory neurons and $j$ excitatory neurons; $\bar{w}_{I \to E}$ is the expected conductance of one synapse, not an identical realised weight. An inhibitory volley gives

   $$
   \Delta g_{\text{inh,post=E} , j} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{active}}} W_{k j}^{I E} ,
   \tag{2}
   $$

   where $\mathcal{I}_{\text{active}}$ is the active inhibitory set and $\Delta g_{\text{inh,post=E} , j}$ the inhibitory conductance increment at E neuron $j$, in μS; both weights and participation therefore enter the increment.

2. **Apply paired pool-size controls.** We swept $N_{I} \in \{${nLo}, {nMid}, {nHi}$\}$. Fixed-summed controls used parent means $\mu_{\text{init}} \in \{${gLo}, {gMid}, {gHi}$\}$ μS, so $\bar{w}_{I \to E} \propto \frac{1}{N_{I}}$; fixed-mean-synapse controls scaled $\mu_{\text{init}}$ with $N_{I}$, giving nominal $\bar{w}_{I \to E} \in \{${jNsAt0}, {jNsAt1}, {jNsAt2}$\}$ nS. Nominal values approximate the post-clamp expectations; the arms coincide at {cfgNIReference} I neurons, with one additional shared condition at {nMid} I neurons and nominal 1 μS summed coupling.

3. **Drive and measure the networks.** Nominal E→I summed coupling stayed at {cfgGEiTotal} μS; input weights used parent mean {rRecipeWInMean} μS, {initialZeroPercent}% initial zeros, compensation for zeroing and fan-in normalization. Each network received {cfgNIn} independent {cfgInputRateHz} Hz input channels, implemented as Bernoulli spikes per {cfgDtMs} ms timestep, for {cfgTMs} ms and {cfgNBatch} trials, with seeds {seeds}. We included all conditions and averaged spike counts over the full duration, trials and neurons within each population; overlapping controls reused measurements, giving {nSeeds} seeds at each of 18 conditions from {conditionSeedCount} distinct simulations. The reanalysis used these rates, not raw spike trains; population rates alone do not establish gamma oscillations \[[1](#ref-1)\].

4. **Display pool-size comparisons.** We displayed the recorded population-rate responses for fixed total and fixed per-synapse coupling with their shared controls and seed aggregation.
