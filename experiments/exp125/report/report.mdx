## Abstract

We trained a small LIF network to classify handwritten digits encoded as brightness-dependent spikes. Training and validation loss decreased. A newly recorded response shows hidden and output activity in the validation-selected model. One training replicate and one illustrative response do not establish robustness across seeds, writers or encoding draws.

## Methods

Architecture and settings appear in [Figure 3](#fig-network) and [Table 1](#architecture-config).

1. **Partition digits.** We normalized the 1,797 scikit-learn 8 × 8 digit images [1] by dividing intensities by 16. Stratified splits reserved 360 test images (seed 42), then 1,077 training and 360 validation images (seed 43). Partitions were image-disjoint, without writer constraints.

2. **Encode inputs.** Each pixel generated independent Bernoulli spikes with probability normalized intensity × 0.25 per 1 ms step, over 50 ms: a maximum 250 Hz discrete rate code, not a fine-grid Poisson approximation. Optimization used fresh draws (seed 45); full-split evaluations reused fixed streams (training/validation/test seeds 52/53/54).

3. **Define the classifier.** Dense signed projections connected 64 inputs, 128 hidden LIF neurons and ten output LIF neurons. Both layers used decay 0.9, threshold 1, delayed subtractive reset and no explicit recurrence or refractory interval. Weights used default fan-in-scaled uniform initialization; biases started at 0.1. States reset per image. Full-presentation output counts supplied class scores, with the lowest digit winning ties; decoding knew image boundaries.

4. **Train and select.** We used initialization seed 42, shuffle seed 44, 50 epochs, batches of 64 and Adam (learning rate 0.001, no weight decay). Cross-entropy gradients used a slope-25 fast-sigmoid surrogate [2] and norm clipping at 1. Complete training/validation splits were evaluated at epochs 0–50 in batches of at most 256. Loss was the image mean; accuracy was the correct fraction. Minimum validation loss selected parameters, choosing the earliest epoch on ties; test images were evaluated once afterward.

5. **Check learning.** We checked finite losses, gradients and selected parameters, changed learned weights, exhaustive disjoint partitions, checkpoint selection and test predictions. One training seed was used; calibration and across-replicate uncertainty were not measured.

6. **Record a response.** We presented the first validation image through the reused selected model with fresh seed-46 encoding, zero initial states and fixed weights. Selection ignored correctness and activity. We recorded input, hidden and output spikes and checked output counts against class scores and prediction.

7. **Calculate autocorrelograms.** SNNLab's [spike_autocorrelogram](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/analysis/#spike_autocorrelogram) measured pooled counts separately for each layer:

   $$
   A_{\mathrm{corr},P}[\ell]
   =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N_t-\ell-1}n_P[k]\,n_P[k+\ell]}
   {(N_t-\ell)\,\bar n_P^{\,2}}.
   \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $P$ identifies hidden or output neurons, $n_P[k]$ is the count in bin $k$, $\bar n_P$ its mean across $N_t=50$ bins, and $A_{\mathrm{corr},P}$ the dimensionless correlation. Bins were 1 ms; integer lags $\ell=1,\ldots,20$ represent 1–20 ms. Independent stationary activity has baseline one. Zero lag was omitted; silent populations give undefined curves. No smoothing, transient removal or trial averaging was applied.
