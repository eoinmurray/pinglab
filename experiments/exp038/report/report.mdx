## Abstract

We asked what happens when reciprocal inhibition is enabled after feedforward training. We held the learned input and readout weights fixed and varied bidirectional E↔I coupling in three MNIST classifiers. At full loop strength, mean E rate fell from {rateOff} to {rateOn} Hz, while test accuracy fell by {accCost} percentage points. This demonstrates post-training rate suppression under these conditions, without isolating a benefit of gamma timing or testing whether retraining could recover accuracy.

## Results

<span id="loc-3" />

### Loop-strength rates and accuracy

At full loop strength, E rate fell from approximately {rateOff} to {rateOn} Hz, I rate reached {inhibitionOn} Hz, and accuracy fell by {accCost} percentage points. Because both coupling directions varied, lower activity alone does not identify a causal benefit of rhythm ([Fig. 1](#loc-4)).

## Methods

We reused networks from the [exp022](/exp022) — [*MNIST*](/exp022) and reanalysed recorded inference observations. No new training or simulation was performed for this account.

1. **Reuse trained classifiers.** MNIST handwritten digits \[[1](#ref-1)\] supplied 6,300 training and 700 validation images from the official training partition. Conductance-based leaky-integrate-and-fire networks had 784 Poisson input channels, 1,024 excitatory (E), 256 inhibitory (I), and 10 output neurons; pixels set rates up to 25 Hz. Input and readout weights trained for 50 epochs; class scores were mean pre-reset output voltages. We selected the minimum mean validation cross-entropy over three fixed encoding draws, breaking ties by accuracy and then earliest epoch, rather than using final-epoch weights.

2. **Enable the loop after training.** Three feedforward controls, seeds 42–44, had no activity penalty or recurrent coupling during training. Dimensionless strength $s$ took eleven values from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.1; it set E→I and I→E initializer means to $s$ and $2 s$, respectively, with standard deviations one tenth of those means and normalization by source population size. These lower-clamped normal weights replaced the zero recurrent matrices; learned input and readout weights stayed fixed, and E→E and I→I coupling stayed zero. The same network seed was reused across strengths; no optimization followed the intervention.

3. **Evaluate responses.** Each strength used the same {evalN} images from the official {evalPool}-image test partition, with 200 ms presentations and 0.1 ms steps. Accuracy counted correct classifications; rates included all neurons and all evaluated presentations:

   $$
   r_{P} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{eval}} N_{P} T_{\text{present}}} \sum_{b = 1}^{N_{\text{eval}}} \sum_{n \in P} n_{\text{spike} ( b , n )} .
   \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $P$ denotes E or I, $N_{P}$ its neuron count, $N_{\text{eval}}$ the number of presentations, $T_{\text{present}}$ their duration in seconds, and $n_{\text{spike} ( b , n )}$ neuron $n$’s spike count during presentation $b$; $r_{P}$ is in hertz. Curves show means and sample standard deviations across the three networks; single-image rasters are illustrative.

4. **Probe input drive.** Auxiliary probes reused seed-42 classifiers trained with and without the loop. Uniform independent Poisson inputs covered 26 rates between 0 and 100 Hz, with 32 trials per rate; a separate trained-loop probe used one test image at ten maximum pixel rates from 0 to approximately 23.08 Hz. These recorded firing curves complement the [exp023](/exp023) — [*Turning the PING Loop On*](/exp023) they are not additional loop-transfer accuracy evaluations.
