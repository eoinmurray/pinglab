## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates the
influence of one population on another ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
This mean-field example isolates A→B excitation and compares its timing with a
matched uncoupled control. Local E/I circuits generate gamma; there is no shared
oscillatory drive, feedback, competing sender or attention cue.

## Discussion

The control distinguishes coupling-induced phase capture from the pre-existing
frequency difference. The timing close-up shows how incoming excitation relates
to receiver inhibition and output. These observations support a CTC-like example
within this model; they do not establish preferential information transmission,
selective attention, lasting locking or repeatability across seeds. The closure
uses a stationary noisy-LIF transfer function, fixed driving forces and population
rates; it omits voltage-dependent shunting and finite-population fluctuations.
