## Abstract

{data ? <p>We inherited trained COBA and PING classifiers and kept their weights fixed. We measured the strength and variability of the inhibition received by excitatory (E) neurons in PING, then built artificial GABA inputs matching those properties and delivered them asynchronously to COBA. For the same digit image, mean E firing fell from {round(data.conditions[0].rate_e_hz, 1)} to {round(data.conditions[2].rate_e_hz, 1)} Hz—about {round(100*(1-data.conditions[2].rate_e_hz/data.conditions[0].rate_e_hz), 0)}% suppression. This single-image result shows that matched asynchronous inhibition can almost eliminate COBA's excitatory activity, although the artificial event rate differed from PING's. A separate small classification test is reported below.</p> : <p>No completed evidence is selected.</p>}

## Methods

These equations summarize the implemented discrete-time procedure for Figure 1's
third panel. Index $j$ labels an E neuron and its private input; $n$ labels one of
$T$ time bins. Bars and $\operatorname{sd}$ denote the temporal mean and standard
deviation over the displayed trial, using divisor $T$.

1. **Measure the PING target.** For its recorded GABA conductance $g^{P}_{j,n}$, set

   <a id="eq-inhibition-targets"></a>

   $$
   \mu_j=\overline{g^{P}_j},\qquad
   \sigma_j=\operatorname{sd}(g^{P}_j). \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $\mu_j$ and $\sigma_j$ are the target conductance mean and SD, in µS.

2. **Generate calibrated Bernoulli event streams.** Draw a separate uniform random stream for every E neuron:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-events"></a>

   $$
   u_{j,n}\sim\mathcal U(0,1),\qquad
   s_{j,n}=\mathbf 1[u_{j,n}<p_j]. \tag{2}
   $$

   The draws $u_{j,n}$ are initially independent; $p_j$ is the per-bin event
   probability (fitted in step 4), $s_{j,n}$ is a binary event, and $\mathbf 1$ is the indicator.

3. **Apply the GABA filter.** Form the unit-amplitude conductance trace $h_{j,n}$:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-filter"></a>

   $$
   h_{j,n}=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{GABA}}h_{j,n-1}+s_{j,n-1}. \tag{3}
   $$

   Here $\Delta t$ is the simulation timestep and $\tau_{GABA}$ the reference GABA
   decay time taken from the PING simulation. Set $h_{j,-1}=s_{j,-1}=0$; the event index implements one timestep
   of delay.

4. **Match both moments.** Adjust $p_j$ to match the coefficient of variation, then scale:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-calibration"></a>

   $$
   \frac{\operatorname{sd}(h_j)}{\overline h_j}
   \approx\frac{\sigma_j}{\mu_j},\qquad
   q_j=\frac{\mu_j}{\overline h_j},\qquad
   g^{A}_{j,n}=q_jh_{j,n}. \tag{4}
   $$

   The positive increment $q_j$ is in µS; $g^A$ is artificial conductance. Resample
   a private stream when necessary to reach 1% SD error. No E response is used.

5. **Deliver inhibition to frozen COBA.** Each stream reaches only its paired E neuron:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-current"></a>

   $$
   I^{\mathrm{inh}}_{j,n}=g^{A}_{j,n}
   \bigl(E_{\mathrm{GABA}}-V_{j,n}\bigr). \tag{5}
   $$

   Here $I^{\mathrm{inh}}$ is the inward-positive inhibitory current, $V$ the E
   membrane voltage and $E_{\mathrm{GABA}}$ the GABA reversal potential. With
   conductance in µS and voltage in mV, current is in nA. Sensory spikes and
   original COBA weights remain unchanged; E activity cannot alter these inputs.


6. **Check classification on 100 test images.** Select 10 images per digit from
   the official MNIST test partition using selection seed 42. Encode pixels at a
   maximum rate of 25 Hz/channel, using seed 402211 plus the original test index;
   reuse exactly the same sensory spikes for all three conditions. Reset network
   state before each 200-ms trial, retaining zero initial conductance and no
   burn-in. Use each original network's frozen final-epoch checkpoint and readout;
   the artificial-input condition uses the same COBA weights as the baseline.
   For each image, first run PING and fit private inputs to its per-E conductance
   mean and SD using steps 1–4, with artificial seed 302211 plus the test index.
   Apply those inputs to COBA. Choose the digit with the largest full-trial mean
   pre-reset readout voltage; labels are used for balanced sampling and scoring,
   never for conductance calibration or parameter fitting. Verify frozen weights,
   native replay, and realised conductance matching. No retraining or test-based
   selection is performed. Because calibration needs PING's response to the same
   image, this is an image-conditioned intervention rather than a standalone
   artificial-inhibition classifier; one seed and one encoder draw per image do
   not establish broad generalisation or isolate synchrony.
