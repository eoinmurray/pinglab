## Question

Does a theta-frequency envelope on otherwise independent afferent excitation organise fast population bursting? We extend the circuit in [Susin and Destexhe (2021)](/exp099) with periodic input and a constant-input comparison. The circuit is a modified conductance-based LIF version of the published PING network.\[[1](#ref-1)\]

Both populations receive excitatory afferents. From the start of the simulation, with no burn-in, the theta condition follows

$$
\lambda(t)=\lambda_0+A\sin(2\pi f_\theta t).
\tag{1}
$$

Here $t$ is time in seconds from initialization, $\lambda(t)$ is the rate of each afferent in spikes/s, $\lambda_0=2$ spikes/s is the mean, $A=1.8$ spikes/s is the modulation amplitude, and $f_\theta=5$ Hz is the envelope frequency. The rate ranges from 0.2 to 3.8 Hz and starts at its rising mean crossing. The comparison remains at 2 Hz. The two conditions therefore have the same prescribed mean over complete cycles; their realised spike counts and neuronal firing rates need not match.

These observations characterise this circuit under the specified input envelopes. They do not establish a separate gamma oscillator: sharp theta-locked bursts can contribute harmonics to the filtered band, and a maximum at a search boundary is not evidence of an isolated spectral peak. Repeated seeds, spectral-background checks and feedback interventions would be needed for a stronger mechanistic claim.

## Methods

1. **Pair the input conditions.** Reuse connectivity, weights and the initial state;
   generate target-specific Poisson counts with the same random streams. Only the
   prescribed source-rate envelope differs. Parameters are listed in the appendix.
2. **Measure population rates and spectra.** Count all spikes in 1 ms bins and divide
   by population size and bin duration. Use 200–800 ms for rate and spectrum summaries.
   Welch spectra use a Hann window, 500 ms segments and 50% overlap; report the
   raw maximum within 30–90 Hz, with 2 Hz bin spacing.
3. **Measure phase concentration.** Apply a fourth-order Butterworth 30–90 Hz
   bandpass in second-order sections forward and backward over the full recording.
   Take the Hilbert-transform magnitude as the amplitude envelope, then crop to
   the measurement interval. Concentration is the magnitude of the amplitude-weighted
   mean unit phase vector at the prescribed 5 Hz phase. The constant condition uses
   the same clock. This is descriptive and has no surrogate correction.
