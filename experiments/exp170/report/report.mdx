## Methods

These equations summarize the implemented discrete-time procedure for Figure 1's
third panel. Index $j$ labels an E neuron and its private input, $n=0,\ldots,N-1$
indexes the $N$ time bins, and $\Delta t$ is the timestep. Bars and
$\operatorname{sd}$ denote temporal mean and SD over the trial, using divisor $N$.

1. **Measure the PING targets and fix the event count.** For recorded inhibitory
   conductance $g^P_{j,n}$, define

   <a id="eq-inhibition-targets"></a>

   $$
   \mu_j=\overline{g^P_j},\qquad
   \sigma_j=\operatorname{sd}(g^P_j),\qquad K=24. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $\mu_j$ and $\sigma_j$ are conductance targets in µS; $K$ is the observed
   number of spikes from each PING I neuron in the displayed 200-ms trial.

2. **Generate exactly $K$ private events.** For event index $k=0,\ldots,K-1$, interpolate:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-events"></a>

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   r_{j,k}&=(k+\phi_j)\frac{N-1}{K},\\
   b_{j,k}&=\operatorname{round}\!\left[(1-\alpha_j)r_{j,k}+\alpha_j c_{j,k}\right],\\
   s_{j,n}&=\mathbf1[n\in\{b_{j,k}\}_k].
   \end{aligned}\tag{2}
   $$

   $\phi_j$ is a random phase sampled independently for each neuron from the uniform distribution on $[0,1)$, $r_{j,k}$ is a regular
   event-bin position, and $c_{j,k}$ is the sorted sequence of $K$ bins sampled
   uniformly without replacement from $0,\ldots,N-2$. The fitted interpolation
   $\alpha_j\in[0,1]$ controls timing irregularity. Rounded bins $b_{j,k}$ remain
   distinct; $s$ is the binary event sequence and $\mathbf1$ the indicator.
   Excluding the last input bin keeps every delayed event inside the trial.

3. **Apply the GABA filter.** Form the unit-amplitude trace $h$:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-filter"></a>

   $$
   h_{j,n}=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\mathrm{GABA}}}h_{j,n-1}+s_{j,n-1}. \tag{3}
   $$

   $\tau_{\mathrm{GABA}}$ is PING's GABA decay time. Set
   $h_{j,-1}=s_{j,-1}=0$; delivery takes one timestep.

4. **Fit timing irregularity and event strength.** Choose $\alpha_j$ and scale:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-calibration"></a>

   $$
   \frac{\operatorname{sd}(h_j)}{\overline h_j}\approx\frac{\sigma_j}{\mu_j},\qquad
   q_j=\frac{\mu_j}{\overline h_j},\qquad g^A_{j,n}=q_jh_{j,n}. \tag{4}
   $$

   $q_j$ is the positive conductance increment in µS and $g^A$ the artificial
   conductance. Resample random timing endpoints when needed to bracket the
   target variability, then bisect $\alpha_j$ and retain its closest match.
   The SD-error tolerance is 1%. Calibration uses no E response and preserves $K$.

5. **Deliver inhibition to frozen COBA.** Each stream reaches only its paired E neuron:

   <a id="eq-inhibition-current"></a>

   $$
   I^{\mathrm{inh}}_{j,n}=g^A_{j,n}\bigl(E_{\mathrm{GABA}}-V_{j,n}\bigr),\qquad
   \lambda_j=\frac{K}{N\Delta t}. \tag{5}
   $$

   $I^{\mathrm{inh}}$ is inward-positive current, $V$ membrane voltage, and
   $E_{\mathrm{GABA}}$ the inhibitory reversal potential. With conductance in µS
   and voltage in mV, current is in nA. $\lambda_j$ is the realised event rate
   (use $\Delta t$ in seconds for hertz). Sensory spikes and learned COBA weights
   remain unchanged; E activity cannot alter these inputs.

These count-constrained, timing-calibrated streams are not Poisson inputs.
There are 1,024 private channels rather than 256 I neurons, and no inhibitory
feedback. The shared zero-conductance onset leaves residual correlation.
Matching source rates and conductance moments does not match voltage-dependent
current, autocorrelation or higher-order timing. COBA and PING have different
learned weights; no retraining, accuracy evaluation or independent replication
was performed.
