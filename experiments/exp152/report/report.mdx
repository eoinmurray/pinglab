## Abstract

Two single-neuron Elevate controllers drive opposing muscles on a rotary joint
with a 2 g tip mass under gravity. Motor spikes activate muscles and inhibit the
peer; angle feedback excites each controller. Three ROS2 Humble nodes exchange
1 ms spike windows in Docker on separate CPUs.

## Control Approach

The current tuning came from an empirical parameter scan: we varied the neurons’
background excitation, sensory feedback strength and muscle response per spike,
then selected a combination that produced stable, repeatable oscillation without
hitting the joint limits. The retired recordings were reported at about
**0.57 Hz with a 63° peak-to-peak excursion**; those measurements are no longer
available for inspection here.

To formalise this, we could add an online adaptive controller that measures each
cycle’s frequency and amplitude, compares them with the desired values, and
gradually adjusts the parameters to reduce both errors. Small parameter
perturbations could reveal their effects during operation, allowing adaptation
to account for their coupling. Updates would need bounds and a slower timescale
than the oscillation to preserve stability; achievable targets and convergence
would require validation.
