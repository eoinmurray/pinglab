## Abstract

## Methods

The executed architecture and simulation settings are listed in [Table 1](#architecture-config). The parameter table reads saved final-training snapshots and that compute run's recorded configuration, following the same selected presentation ancestry as the figures. Missing snapshots are shown as unavailable; current recipe settings and old exported table rows are not substituted.

1. **Prepare SHD.** We split 8,156 official training utterances [1] (20 classes, 700 channels) into 7,340 training and 816 validation examples using a seed-42 per-class shuffle. Original spike-train lengths varied: last-spike times ranged from 0.233 to 1.369 s. We kept only events before 1 s to fit the simulation, flooring times into binary 1 ms bins. Speakers could overlap between splits; the official test set was unused.

2. **Corrupt inputs.** Per channel and 1 ms bin, we deleted existing spikes with probability 0.0008 and filled empty bins with probability 0.0016 (approximately 1.6 Hz). Both models shared example order and noise draws; validation noise stayed fixed across epochs for consistent comparison. Corruption exposed models to missing and extra spikes, but its benefit was not tested against uncorrupted training.

3. **Initialize hidden weights.** Both models shared seed-42 learned matrices. SNNLab's [LowerClampedNormal](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/lang/parameters/#lowerclampednormal) used parent mean 0.09 μS and SD 0.009 μS. Input→E1 and E1→E2 initial zero fractions were 0.95 and 0.90, followed by division by retained fan-in (35 and 12.8). Negative draws were clamped; initial zeros remained trainable and weights remained nonnegative.

4. **Initialize readout and feedback.** Signed E2→readout weights had Gaussian mean zero and SD 0.1 μS, centered across source neurons for each class, without sparsification or fan-in scaling. PING feedback was fixed at $0.05/128$ μS per E→I edge and $0.05/32$ μS per I→E edge: 0.05 μS summed incoming conductance in either direction. COBA had no inhibitory feedback.

5. **Score utterances.** We used a custom rate-normalized mean-voltage score:

   $$
   z_c=G\,\bar u_c\,\frac{r_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\max(\bar r_{E2},r_{\mathrm{floor}})}. \tag{1}
   $$

   Here $z_c$ is the score for class $c$, $\bar u_c$ its mean non-spiking readout voltage, and $\bar r_{E2}$ the per-neuron E2 rate, both over the full second. We used gain $G=20$, reference rate $r_{\mathrm{ref}}=20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and rate floor $r_{\mathrm{floor}}=1\,\mathrm{Hz}$, predicting the largest score. The positive scaling preserves that choice for fixed weights but changes cross-entropy and training gradients.

6. **Train both models.** We used SNNLab's [train](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/sim/execution-functions/#train) for 50 epochs with class-stratified batches of 256, merging tails smaller than 20. AdamW with zero weight decay minimized mean cross-entropy per represented class; input/interlayer/readout learning rates were $5\times10^{-6}/2\times10^{-5}/0.01$, with betas 0.9/0.999 and no weight decay. The [FastSigmoid](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/lang/training/#fastsigmoid) surrogate used slope $100/15\,\mathrm{mV}^{-1}$ and gradient scale 0.01. States reset per utterance. Reported losses were example means; validation used epoch-end weights, including epoch zero.

7. **Record matched responses.** Using SNNLab's [simulate](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/sim/execution-functions/#simulate), we replayed the first validation utterance with final-epoch weights, one shared fresh corruption draw (seed 46) and no updates. Selection used validation split order, not original dataset index, prediction or spike appearance.

8. **Calculate autocorrelograms.** We applied SNNLab's [spike_autocorrelogram](https://snnlab.eoinmurray.info/api/analysis/#spike_autocorrelogram) separately to each population over the full utterance:

   $$
   A_{\mathrm{corr},P}[\ell]
   =\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N_t-\ell-1}n_P[k]\,n_P[k+\ell]}
   {(N_t-\ell)\,\bar n_P^{\,2}}.
   \tag{2}
   $$

   Here $A_{\mathrm{corr},P}$ is dimensionless autocorrelation for population $P$ (E or I in one layer), $n_P[k]$ its count in bin $k$, and $\bar n_P$ its mean across $N_t=1000$ bins. We used 1 ms bins and integer lags $\ell=1,\ldots,200$ (1–200 ms). Independent stationary activity has baseline one; zero lag is omitted, and silent or too-short recordings give undefined curves. No smoothing, transient removal or trial averaging was applied; the stimulus-evoked activity envelope contributes to the correlation.
