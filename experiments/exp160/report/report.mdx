## Introduction

This experiment implements the one-way [mean-field example](/exp159) with
conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire neurons and private stochastic sensory
inputs. Communication through coherence motivates inspecting incoming excitation
relative to receiver inhibition and output
([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)). The experiment tests
finite-time entrainment; it has no competing sender, attention cue or readout.

## Discussion

The uncoupled rhythms drift apart; the selected one-way coupling brings receiver
frequency close to the sender's and stabilises relative phase over the measured
window. This is compatible with CTC-like entrainment in this network. It does not
establish selective attention, an optimal excitability phase, causal transmission
of an encoded signal, or lasting phase locking. Unlike the mean-field closure,
the simulation retains heterogeneous connections, finite-population spike timing,
membrane voltage, refractoriness and voltage-dependent synaptic driving forces.
