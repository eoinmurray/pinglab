## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates influence
between neuronal populations ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
Here the competing inputs carry distinct spatial patterns rather than only
oscillatory activity. The sensory strength across sender E cells follows a
Gaussian plus a uniform pedestal. Both patterns remain present and fixed while a
separate cue changes which sender is favoured. Receiver output is measured directly
as a spatial firing-rate profile, without a trained readout.

## Discussion

C expresses both source patterns, with the cued pattern stronger in each epoch.
The original phase and spatial criteria are unchanged. The modified condition
uses 400 E / 100 I cells per circuit, independent 128-source sensory/cue masks,
local E→I fan-in 80 and pooled sender→C I fan-in 40. E target gain CV is 0.1
in A/B and zero in C; I gain CV is 0.2. Private stochastic input still distinguishes
C E cells. Local E→I and I→I strengths are multiplied by 0.8 and 0.1.
Sensory contrast is 0.08; C background is 28 Hz/channel. Each sender supplies
0.6 µS nominal incoming excitation to C E and 0.39 µS to C I.

{data?.rasters && <p>Mean E participation per complete cycle ranges from {round(100*Math.min(...data.rasters.flatMap(r => ['A_E','B_E','C_E'].map(k => r.populations[k].mean_cycle_participation))),1)}% to {round(100*Math.max(...data.rasters.flatMap(r => ['A_E','B_E','C_E'].map(k => r.populations[k].mean_cycle_participation))),1)}% across the measured circuits and epochs. I recruitment remains high, although its spike trains are distinct. This is partial recruitment in a rhythmic network, not evidence of biological realism.</p>}

Changes were selected jointly through exploratory iteration. Spatial selection is
weaker than in the older homogeneous condition; no independent seed validation
or causal attribution to individual changes was performed. Source rate, frequency
and timing all change under the cue, so timing has not been isolated as the cause
of spatial output. There is no classifier, arbitrary-pattern test or
rate-preserving timing intervention. Finite measurement windows do not establish
asymptotic locking.
