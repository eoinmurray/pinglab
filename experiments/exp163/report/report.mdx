## Introduction

Communication through coherence proposes that rhythmic timing regulates influence
between neuronal populations ([Fries, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)).
Here the competing inputs carry distinct spatial patterns rather than only
oscillatory activity. The sensory strength across sender E cells follows a
Gaussian plus a uniform pedestal. Both patterns remain present and fixed while a
separate cue changes which sender is favoured. Receiver output is measured directly
as a spatial firing-rate profile, without a trained readout.

## Discussion

C expresses both source patterns, with the cued pattern stronger. The selected
cue increases contrast largely through lower distractor-region output while the
selected-region response remains near its previous level. Source rate, frequency
and timing can all change under the cue, so the experiment demonstrates spatial
output selection without establishing timing as its cause. The centre-swap
control excludes a fixed spatial preference within this symmetric model but does
not establish robustness across input seeds. Cells retain homogeneous dynamics,
weights are structured deterministically, and synchronous spike counts produce
stepped spatial rate profiles. There is no classifier, arbitrary-pattern test,
rate-preserving timing intervention or claim of sustained locking beyond the
measured windows.
