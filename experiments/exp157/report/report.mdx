## Abstract

We test competing gamma inputs after narrowing autonomous frequency targets to
34/36/38 Hz for A/C/B. Only inhibitory population response times are retuned;
all weights and E-only drive schedules stay fixed.

## Methods

### 1. Native rate–conductance model

SNNLab's `RateConductance` closure reduces the compiled three-circuit COBA network
to a deterministic population model. The equations are read from the native
`model.json` in the selected presentation's compute ancestry, using the same
evidence as Figure 1 and the parameter table. Mean incoming couplings sum effective
conductance jumps over sources and average over target neurons, including absent
self-edges. Reference neuron counts define these sums; no spiking simulation is run.

<MeanFieldModel />

The noisy-LIF gain uses imported membrane, threshold, reset and refractory
parameters, with {cfg && `${cfg.voltage_noise_mv} mV voltage-noise scale and ${cfg.quadrature_nodes}-node quadrature`}.
Rate-relaxation times are phenomenological closure choices. Fixed resting driving
forces omit conductance-dependent shunting; the closure also omits input covariance
and finite-population fluctuations. See the [mean-field note](/notes/mean-field-model)
for the gain derivation and closure assumptions.

### 2. Calibration, drive protocol and integration

Only I rate-relaxation times are calibrated to the autonomous A/C/B targets of
34/36/38 Hz, using uncoupled constant inputs at the inherited hold levels. A bracketed
Brent root search matches E-rate peak-interval frequencies over 1500–2500 ms;
brackets and tolerances are retained in the compute configuration. Weights, E
relaxation times and drive levels are inherited without firing-rate recalibration.
These finite-window targets do not fix frequency during the ramps.

Figure 1 then starts from zero rates and conductances, with no warm-up or resets.
Nine 500 ms windows hold all inputs off, ramp C, hold C, ramp A, hold A, ramp B,
hold B, ramp B again, then hold all inputs. Ramps are linear; earlier inputs remain
at their attained levels. The uncoupled C control repeats the same initial state
and drive schedule with both feedforward gains zero.
