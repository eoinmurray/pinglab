## Appendix A — Model specialisation and stability

### A1 — Experimental specialisation

The [mean-field note](/notes/mean-field-model#the-model) defines the model and
its [derivation](/notes/mean-field-model#derivation). This experiment disables
E→E and I→I coupling and has no external conductance input. Instead,
$I_{\mathrm{ext},E}$ is the swept tonic current and $I_{\mathrm{ext},I}=0$.
The [four-state reduction](/notes/mean-field-model#four-state-reductions)
therefore uses $x=(r_E,r_I,g_e^I,g_i^E)$.

Rates are spikes per neuron per ms. The decay times are $\tau_{E\to I}=2$ ms
and swept $\tau_{I\to E}$; the resting-voltage driving forces are
$\Delta V_{\mathrm{exc},Q}=65$ mV and $\Delta V_{\mathrm{inh},Q}=15$ mV for
both populations $Q\in\{E,I\}$. The fixed noise scale is $\sigma_{V,Q}=4$ mV;
rate-relaxation times are 20/5 ms for E/I. Recorded values and numerical settings
are listed in the parameter table.

The note's matrix sums denote this experiment's
imported reference couplings, not newly sampled matrices. The tonic current
is a control parameter; no empirical input-rate mapping is inferred.
The general [gain](/notes/mean-field-model#derivation-firing-gain),
[fixed-driving-force approximation](/notes/mean-field-model#from-conductances-to-effective-currents)
and [rate relaxation](/notes/mean-field-model#derivation-rate-relaxation)
are not rederived here.
