Pinglab Paper Archive

This library examines how circuits generate gamma and whether it contributes to brain computation, including criticism and competing explanations. It prioritises work that helps simulations distinguish gamma’s effects from firing rate, inhibition, connectivity, and shared input.

Generation & Dynamics

How circuits generate gamma: PING, ING, interneuron physiology, synaptic kinetics, heterogeneity, delays, and transitions between rhythmic and asynchronous states.

Proposed Functions

Proposed roles for gamma in communication, routing, attention, sensory coding, inference, memory, learning, and plasticity.

Experimental Tests

Recordings, perturbations, and behavioural studies that support or challenge gamma's proposed functions, including null results and failures to replicate.

Criticism & Competing Explanations

Objections to functional theories, gamma as a by-product or correlate, limits of phase-based communication, and explanations based on rates, excitability, or common drive.

Modelling & Measurement

Spiking and population models, relationships between spikes and field signals, synchrony measures, analytical artefacts, and criticisms of model assumptions.

Biological Constraints

Circuit architecture, realistic inputs, variability, behavioural timescales, and energetic costs that constrain proposed gamma mechanisms.

History of Gamma

Landmark discoveries and historical reviews tracing gamma research from early olfactory recordings to cortical synchrony, binding debates, inhibitory mechanisms, attention, and causal tests.
