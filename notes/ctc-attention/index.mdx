<TextSlide title="CTC and Attention" density="compact">

### Hypothesis

Communication through coherence (CTC) proposes that gamma synchronisation controls
which inputs influence a receiver, without changing anatomical connections.
Incoming volleys are most effective during its excitable phase \[[1](#ref-1)\].

### Mechanism: competing A/B inputs → receiver C

1. Attention strengthens or speeds A's gamma rhythm, favouring an earlier volley at C.
2. A excites C and recruits local inhibition, reducing B's later influence.
3. Entrainment aligns subsequent A volleys with C's excitable windows; a reset can reopen competition. Timing depends on transmission delays and circuit dynamics.

### Proposed test: classify the attended digit

1. Present two simultaneous 8×8 digits to A and B; cue which digit to classify. A ten-class readout receives only C's activity.
2. Split source images before pairing. Test held-out pairs under both cues with identical sensory spikes: predictions should follow the cue.
3. Freeze weights and test coupling, phase-dependent transmission, and timing disruption/rescue, controlling firing rates and gamma power.

This adapts Bosman's competing-input motif \[[2](#ref-2)\]. Classification or coherence alone does not establish CTC.

</TextSlide>

<TextSlide title="Three tests of CTC">

<span id="testing-ctc" />

Test whether gamma timing contributes to selective routing:

1. **Cue-dependent coupling.** With identical sensory inputs, changing the cue
   should switch both C's preferred gamma coupling and its classification.
   Compare with trial-shuffled controls, matching firing rates and gamma power.
2. **Phase-dependent transmission.** Identical weak probes should evoke different
   responses at different phases of C's gamma cycle. The cued sender's volleys
   should arrive preferentially at high-gain phases.
3. **Timing disruption and rescue.** Disrupt attended-input timing while preserving
   spike counts and within-volley synchrony, then restore it. Selection should
   deteriorate and recover beyond effects seen with sham, distractor and
   rate-matched nonoscillatory controls.


### Interpretation

Classification or coherence alone cannot establish CTC.
Rate-based solutions, shared inputs and perturbation side effects require
controls. Positive results would support CTC in this model, not establish the
brain's attention mechanism.

</TextSlide>
