Abstract

A capacitor separates electric charge onto two plates. We follow electrons as it charges, holds its charge and discharges, connecting their redistribution to voltage and current. The circuit animation uses schematic electron dots. We then follow a presynaptic spike as it opens a synapse and triggers a neuron's ionic response.

Introduction

A capacitor has two metal plates separated by an insulator. Charging removes electrons from one plate and adds them to the other. The plates acquire equal and opposite net charges; electrons travel through the external wires, never across the insulating gap in this ideal model.[1]

Capacitance CCC, measured in farads (F), relates the upper plate’s positive charge QQQ, in coulombs (C), to the voltage difference VCV_CVC​, in volts (V):

Charging slows as VCV_CVC​ approaches VbV_bVb​. Hold disconnects the external path; Discharge joins the plates through the resistor. Electron drift runs opposite to conventional current. The time constant τ\tauτ, in seconds, sets the charging and discharging timescale:

A biophysical neuron

A neuron’s membrane separates conducting fluids and acts as a capacitor. Here the moving charges are ions: sodium (Na+\mathrm{Na}^{+}Na+) entering the cell can raise its voltage, while potassium (K+\mathrm{K}^{+}K+) leaving can lower it. Voltage-dependent channels control these flows. In a biological synapse, an arriving presynaptic spike causes transmitter release, opening synaptic channels. We add a standard conductance-based synapse with instantaneous opening and exponential decay, as described in Neuronal Dynamics, Section 3.1, Equations (3.1)–(3.2). This is an extension to the original Hodgkin–Huxley membrane model.[5] Its conductance density gsyng_{\mathrm{syn}}gsyn​ is in mS/cm², and we write its current as inward-positive (the opposite sign convention to that reference):

Isyn=gsyn(Esyn−Vm).(11)I_{\mathrm{syn}}=g_{\mathrm{syn}}(E_{\mathrm{syn}}-V_m). \tag{11}Isyn​=gsyn​(Esyn​−Vm​).(11)

Voltage and the three gates are integrated with fourth-order Runge–Kutta steps of 0.01 ms. Synaptic conductance decays exponentially, evaluated at each step’s start, midpoint and end. Action potentials emerge from the channel dynamics, without a firing threshold or voltage reset. Adjust capacitance, synaptic strength or the available channels to explore the response.
