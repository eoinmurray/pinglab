Abstract

We asked whether inhibitory population size alters PING activity by adding inhibition or merely redistributing a fixed inhibitory budget. We compared pool-size sweeps that held either summed inhibition or individual synaptic strength fixed.

Firing rates stayed stable under fixed total inhibition but fell as the pool grew when individual synaptic strength was preserved. This supports inverse scaling as a compensation rule for this regime; it does not establish uniqueness or demonstrate gamma rhythmicity.
