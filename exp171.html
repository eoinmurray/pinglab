Abstract

We fine-tune COBA for 10 epochs with PING-calibrated private inhibition across 6 matched TR02 activity ceilings. We measure test accuracy and E firing on 1,000 official MNIST test images for one seed.

Methods

These quantities apply separately to each image and recipient E neuron jjj.

Parameters taken from the teacher PING

  1. μj\mu_jμj​: temporal mean of inhibitory conductance arriving at E neuron jjj.
  2. vjv_jvj​: temporal variance of that inhibitory conductance.

Derived parameters for artificial I

  1. qjq_jqj​: conductance jump per artificial event,

    qj=(1+β)vjμj.q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j}.qj​=μj​(1+β)vj​​.

  2. λj\lambda_jλj​: artificial Poisson event rate,

    λj=μj2(1−β)Δt(1+β)vj.\lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)} {\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}.λj​=Δt(1+β)vj​μj2​(1−β)​.

Here β=e−Δt/τI\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}β=e−Δt/τI​, where Δt=0.1\Delta t=0.1Δt=0.1 ms is the timestep and τI=6\tau_I=6τI​=6 ms is the inhibitory decay time. Use seconds for Δt\Delta tΔt; λj\lambda_jλj​ is in events/s. Zero-mean channels receive qj=λj=0q_j=\lambda_j=0qj​=λj​=0.

What is matched

  1. Mean inhibitory conductance: E[gj]=μj\mathbb{E}[g_j]=\mu_jE[gj​]=μj​.
  2. Inhibitory conductance variance: Var⁡(gj)=vj\operatorname{Var}(g_j)=v_jVar(gj​)=vj​.

Here gjg_jgj​ is artificial inhibitory conductance. Matching holds in stationary expectation; finite trials fluctuate. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and correlations are not matched.

Detailed procedure

We fitted each image and recipient E neuron separately.

  1. Measure teacher inhibition. For each E neuron, measure the frozen PING teacher’s conductance mean and variance over 200 ms:

    μj=mean⁡ngjP[n],vj=var⁡ngjP[n].(1)\mu_j=\operatorname{mean}_n g_j^{P}[n], \qquad v_j=\operatorname{var}_n g_j^{P}[n]. \tag{1}μj​=meann​gjP​[n],vj​=varn​gjP​[n].(1)

    Here jjj indexes E neurons, nnn indexes timesteps, and gPg^PgP is teacher inhibitory conductance; μ\muμ and vvv are its temporal mean and variance. Teacher I spike counts were recorded but not fitted. Teacher and student received the same sensory spike train.

  2. Fit artificial input. Solve for event amplitude qjq_jqj​ and Poisson rate λj\lambda_jλj​:

    qj=(1+β)vjμj,λj=μj2(1−β)Δt(1+β)vj.(2)q_j=\frac{(1+\beta)v_j}{\mu_j}, \qquad \lambda_j=\frac{\mu_j^2(1-\beta)}{\Delta t(1+\beta)v_j}. \tag{2}qj​=μj​(1+β)vj​​,λj​=Δt(1+β)vj​μj2​(1−β)​.(2)

    These match the filter’s stationary mean and variance; Appendix A derives equation (2).

    The fixed decay factor was

    β=e−Δt/τI.(3)\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}. \tag{3}β=e−Δt/τI​.(3)

    Here qjq_jqj​ is the conductance jump per event and λj\lambda_jλj​ the rate in events/s. The timestep is Δt=0.1\Delta t=0.1Δt=0.1 ms and GABA decay time τI=6\tau_I=6τI​=6 ms; use seconds when calculating rates. Zero-mean targets receive no events.

  3. Generate artificial inhibition. Give each E neuron an independent Poisson channel, filtered through GABA with a one-timestep delay:

    Nj[n]∼Poisson⁡(λjΔt),gj[n]=βgj[n−1]+qjNj[n−1].(4)N_j[n]\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_j\Delta t), \qquad g_j[n]=\beta g_j[n-1]+q_jN_j[n-1]. \tag{4}Nj​[n]∼Poisson(λj​Δt),gj​[n]=βgj​[n−1]+qj​Nj​[n−1].(4)

    Here NNN counts events and ggg is artificial inhibitory conductance. Its stationary moments are

    E[gj]=qjλjΔt1−β=μj,Var⁡(gj)=qj2λjΔt1−β2=vj.(5)\mathbb{E}[g_j] =\frac{q_j\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta}=\mu_j, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(g_j) =\frac{q_j^2\lambda_j\Delta t}{1-\beta^2}=v_j. \tag{5}E[gj​]=1−βqj​λj​Δt​=μj​,Var(gj​)=1−β2qj2​λj​Δt​=vj​.(5)

    Fitted rates and amplitudes stayed frozen during fine-tuning; events were redrawn each epoch. Mean and variance match in stationary expectation, with finite-trial fluctuations. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and temporal or cross-neuron correlations are not matched. Biological I is silent; artificial inhibition has no E-to-I feedback.

    A 60-ms inhibition-only prehistory initialised conductance. Neurons started fresh at stimulus onset. Test-image fits used frozen PING passes without labels or weight updates. COBA/PING test scores came from TR02.

A. Derivations

For each image and recipient E neuron jjj, fit a conductance jump qjq_jqj​ and Poisson rate λj\lambda_jλj​ (events/s) to the teacher’s conductance moments. Both stay fixed during training. Equation (2) follows from exact stationary moment matching for the discrete filter below.

Suppress the neuron index jjj temporarily. Let g[n]g[n]g[n] be the artificial inhibitory conductance at timestep nnn, and N[n]N[n]N[n] the independent Poisson event count:

g[n]=βg[n−1]+qN[n−1],N[n]∼Poisson⁡(λΔt).(A1)g[n]=\beta g[n-1]+qN[n-1], \qquad N[n]\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda\Delta t). \tag{A1}g[n]=βg[n−1]+qN[n−1],N[n]∼Poisson(λΔt).(A1)

Here Δt\Delta tΔt and inhibitory decay time τI\tau_IτI​ are in seconds; β=e−Δt/τI\beta=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_I}β=e−Δt/τI​ is the fixed decay factor. Events arrive one timestep later. Poisson counts have equal mean and variance:

E[N[n]]=Var⁡(N[n])=λΔt.(A2)\mathbb{E}[N[n]]=\operatorname{Var}(N[n])=\lambda\Delta t. \tag{A2}E[N[n]]=Var(N[n])=λΔt.(A2)

Let μ\muμ and vvv be the teacher’s temporal conductance mean and variance. Matching the stationary mean gives

μ=βμ+qλΔt⟹μ=qλΔt1−β.(A3)\mu=\beta\mu+q\lambda\Delta t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu=\frac{q\lambda\Delta t}{1-\beta}. \tag{A3}μ=βμ+qλΔt⟹μ=1−βqλΔt​.(A3)

New counts are independent of previous conductance, so their covariance is zero. Matching the stationary variance gives

v=β2v+q2λΔt⟹v=q2λΔt1−β2.(A4)v=\beta^2v+q^2\lambda\Delta t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=\frac{q^2\lambda\Delta t}{1-\beta^2}. \tag{A4}v=β2v+q2λΔt⟹v=1−β2q2λΔt​.(A4)

For μ>0\mu>0μ>0 and v>0v>0v>0, dividing variance by mean yields

vμ=q1+β⟹q=(1+β)vμ.(A5)\frac{v}{\mu}=\frac{q}{1+\beta} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=\frac{(1+\beta)v}{\mu}. \tag{A5}μv​=1+βq​⟹q=μ(1+β)v​.(A5)

Substituting this amplitude into the mean equation gives

λ=μ(1−β)qΔt=μ2(1−β)Δt(1+β)v.(A6)\lambda=\frac{\mu(1-\beta)}{q\Delta t} =\frac{\mu^2(1-\beta)}{\Delta t(1+\beta)v}. \tag{A6}λ=qΔtμ(1−β)​=Δt(1+β)vμ2(1−β)​.(A6)

Restore jjj to obtain equation (2): two teacher moments set two artificial-input parameters at fixed decay. Zero-mean targets receive no events. The fit rejects constant positive targets (μ>0\mu>0μ>0, v=0v=0v=0), which finite-rate Poisson input cannot reproduce.

Matching is exact in stationary expectation; finite trials fluctuate. The 60-ms inhibition-only prehistory reduces the initial transient. Teacher spike rate, timing, phase and correlations remain unconstrained.
