Abstract

We plan to compare two-layer conductance-based networks that recognize gestures represented by visual spike events. COBA provides the feedforward baseline; PING adds local excitatory–inhibitory feedback. Matched training and held-out responses will test whether the proposed networks learn and propagate activity.

Training and response measurements remain unperformed.

A single training seed and small validation set cannot establish competitive test performance or a general benefit from inhibition.

Results

COBA training

We plan to train the feedforward COBA baseline for twenty epochs on 108 gesture examples and evaluate 11 examples from separate subjects. Training has not been executed, so learning, weight changes and training time remain unmeasured.

PING training

We plan to train the PING candidate on the same examples, initial feedforward/readout weights and shuffled sample schedule as COBA, with a separate GPU for each model. Its inhibitory populations and four additional learned kernels change both population size and parameter count. Training has not been executed; oscillations and any validation advantage remain unverified.

COBA two-layer response

We will replay the lexicographically first selected validation example through the final COBA weights, without optimizer updates. Selection will not depend on correct classification or raster appearance. No response has yet been measured; one example will illustrate activity propagation rather than summarize performance across gestures.

PING two-layer response

We will replay the identical validation input through the final PING weights, using the same presentation duration and excitatory-neuron display selection as COBA. The paired response will illustrate the proposed feedback circuit; one presentation cannot establish a mechanism for a training difference or sustained gamma activity.

Methods

The architecture and simulation settings are listed in Table 1. We follow the paired training-curve and held-out-response design of exp124 — SHD, with the following gesture-specific protocol. All training and response procedures remain planned.

  1. Prepare gesture events. We will use the training-only Tonic distribution of DVS Gesture [1, 2], containing 1,077 segmented examples with floating-point timestamps in milliseconds. Fractional timestamps are preserved until binning. The two highest-numbered training subjects will supply one validation example per class. Selection seed 135 chooses 108 training examples from the remaining subjects, with ten examples in nine classes and nine in the remaining two classes. The official test set will remain unused.

  2. Encode 500 ms crops. We retain separate polarities, integer-divide sensor coordinates by four to obtain 32 × 32 resolution, and form binary occupancy in 1 ms bins. Repeated events in a bin collapse to one spike. Complete source event extents are retained in the dataset snapshot. At each training epoch, a seeded random integer-millisecond start selects a 500 ms window within each gesture. Both networks receive matching crops under the same batch schedule. Validation uses a fixed centred 500 ms window per example; examples shorter than 500 ms receive zero-input padding. This development validation does not measure whole-gesture or multi-window accuracy. Crop encoding uses indexed, vectorised event selection through SNNLab’s registered encoder interface.

  3. Initialize feedforward weights. Both networks have 16 excitatory maps at 32 × 32 followed by 32 maps at 16 × 16, with 3 × 3 AMPA convolutions and two 2 × 2 average pools. Pooling carries fractional synaptic drive. Initial conductance weights use Gaussian parent mean 0.02 μS and SD 0.004 μS, lower-clamped at zero, without fan-in normalization. Parameter-local draws with training seed 42 make shared kernels identical across models; we will check this equality from the initial weights.

  4. Initialize readout and feedback. Both models have 11 non-spiking leaky-integrator readouts, with signed Gaussian weights of mean zero and SD 0.1, identically initialized. PING adds 4 and 8 inhibitory maps and learned reciprocal 1 × 1 AMPA/GABA kernels with 1 ms delays. E→I and I→E Gaussian parent means are 0.05 and 0.10 μS, respectively, with SD one fifth of the mean and lower clamping at zero. Excitatory/inhibitory passive time constants are 20/5 ms. These operating weights and timestep remain uncalibrated.

  5. Score each gesture. Classification uses the mean voltage of each non-spiking readout over the 500 ms crop:

  6. Train the paired networks. We will train for twenty epochs with the same shuffled example schedule, batch size 54 selected by GPU benchmarking, AdamW, learning rate 0.001, zero weight decay, gradient clipping at 1.0 and fast-sigmoid surrogate slope 1. Native neuron kernels use compilation. All projection weights are trainable; conductance weights remain nonnegative. At batch 54, each epoch contains two optimizer updates; twenty epochs therefore contain forty updates. Hidden, readout and synaptic states reset between crops. Both models use voltage-gradient damping divisor 80 for hidden-neuron voltage updates. Training metrics average pre-update measurements across examples; validation uses fixed epoch-end weights. The earliest completed epoch with minimum example-average validation cross-entropy is selected separately for each model. Training will run concurrently on separate GPUs.

  7. Record matched responses. We will replay the lexicographically first selected validation example through each final-epoch network, without optimizer updates, and verify identical sample inputs and duration. This prespecified sample does not depend on class, correctness or spike appearance. We will record every hidden neuron’s spikes and the class scores. Final weights are used irrespective of the validation-selected epoch.
