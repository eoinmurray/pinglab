Abstract

An arriving spike opens synaptic channels. Their conductance then decays, while current through them changes the receiving neuron's voltage. We use a simple animation to explore how synaptic weight, decay time and reversal potential shape this response, then combine excitatory and inhibitory inputs.

Introduction

A spike causes neurotransmitter release. The transmitter crosses the synaptic cleft and opens channels embedded in the receiving neuron’s membrane. We represent their combined conductance by gsyng_{\mathrm{syn}}gsyn​, measured in nanosiemens (nS). Each arrival at time tjt_jtj​ adds the synaptic weight weventw_{\mathrm{event}}wevent​; between arrivals, conductance decays with time constant τsyn\tau_{\mathrm{syn}}τsyn​. Time ttt and τsyn\tau_{\mathrm{syn}}τsyn​ are in milliseconds (ms), and jjj labels spikes. The Dirac impulse δ(t−tj)\delta(t-t_j)δ(t−tj​) has unit area and units of inverse milliseconds; each impulse adds weventw_{\mathrm{event}}wevent​ nS at arrival.[1]

Open channels carry current according to the difference between their reversal potential EsynE_{\mathrm{syn}}Esyn​ and the neuron’s voltage VmV_mVm​, both in millivolts (mV):

Explore the synapse

Increase Weight weventw_{\mathrm{event}}wevent​ to open more channels, or Decay τsyn\tau_{\mathrm{syn}}τsyn​ to keep them open longer. Move Reversal EsynE_{\mathrm{syn}}Esyn​ above or below −65 mV to raise or lower the neuron’s voltage. At −65 mV, channels still open but the neuron stays at rest. Reversal potential changes the voltage response, not the conductance trace. The default weight of 8 nS produces output spikes; the voltage trace reaches threshold and resets.

Excitatory and inhibitory inputs

Two presynaptic neurons now feed the receiving neuron: E supplies excitation and I supplies inhibition. Each has its own conductance, gEg_EgE​ or gIg_IgI​, and nonnegative synaptic weight, wEw_EwE​ or wIw_IwI​, all in nS. Their conductances obey the same decay-and-arrival rule:

dgPdt=−gPτP+wP∑jδ(t−tjP).(5)\frac{dg_P}{dt}=-\frac{g_P}{\tau_P}+w_P\sum_j\delta(t-t_j^P). \tag{5}dtdgP​​=−τP​gP​​+wP​j∑​δ(t−tjP​).(5)

EEE_EEE​ and EIE_IEI​ are the two reversal potentials, adjustable in Figure 2 and initially set to 0 and −75 mV; capacitance, leak and rest retain their values from equation (3). These are illustrative settings. The two channel symbols represent two distinct conductance components, each combining the physical channels activated by its input.[1]

Increase Weight wEw_EwE​ to strengthen excitation, or Weight wIw_IwI​ to strengthen inhibition. Set either to zero to remove its current. At its default reversal, the inhibitory conductance pulls voltage towards −75 mV and can suppress output spikes. Inhibition therefore uses a positive conductance with a different reversal potential, rather than a negative weight. The default weights are wE=8w_E=8wE​=8 nS and wI=4w_I=4wI​=4 nS, which allow the receiving neuron to fire. Its threshold and reset follow equation (4). Changing EIE_IEI​ can change the polarity of the I-pathway current: it becomes depolarising when EI>VmE_I>V_mEI​>Vm​.
